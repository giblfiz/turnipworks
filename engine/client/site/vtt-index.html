VTT comparison index

Last updated 1 November 2026

A virtual tabletop (VTT) is the software a group uses to play a tabletop roleplaying game together online: a shared map, tokens or miniatures, dice, and usually a way to hide what the players haven't found yet. This page compares 118 of them, and a few related tools, by the features people ask about: the established products, and the new and indie ones their makers have posted on r/VTT, the Reddit community for virtual tabletops.

Who made this table. We make Turnip Works, one of the VTTs below. This table is our own development checklist, so expect our row to fill up over time; it is scored by the same rules as every other row, by what's on our live site. How to read it

Our row is scored by the same rules as everyone else's, and every row is checked on the same schedule. A feature of ours that is built but only on our development server, not yet on the live site, is mentioned in the note as "dev (not yet live)" and does not count until it is live.

Other good reasons to pick a VTT, such as official licensed adventures, campaign organisation, voice and video, or a big community library, aren't columns yet; they show up in each product's upsides.

Each cell says yes, partial, no, n/a (not applicable to that kind of tool) or ?. A ? means we don't know: we didn't find it, which is not the same as it being missing. A value marked (?) is one we couldn't confirm from a source. Values describe the product as people can get it today: planned or unreleased work is in the note, not the value. A product that isn't open to the public yet is described from its maker's own account, and its Stage says so. An official extension from the maker counts as built in; a third-party add-on counts as partial. Open a product's name for the details, with a link to where each claim came from. We did not test every product by hand. The established products were checked against their own sites and documentation. The new and indie rows come from their makers' Reddit posts and their sites as read on 24 October 2026; since then we checked only whether each site is still up. Each row shows both dates.

What the columns mean

2D / 3D
Whether the table is a flat map seen from above (2D), a 3D scene you can turn and tilt (3D), or a flat map with 3D figures or terrain on it, or a switch between both (2D+3D). 3D dice alone don't count here: they have their own column.
Runs on
Where it runs: in a web browser tab, as an installed desktop app (Windows, Mac, Linux), on phones or tablets (iOS, Android), or in a VR headset.
Price
What the game master (GM, who runs the game) pays. free: no paid tier at all. free tier: usable free, with paid upgrades. one-time: a purchase. subscription: a monthly or yearly fee. The note says what players pay.
Accounts
Who must sign up before playing. none: nobody (a link is enough). gm: only the game master. everyone: players too.
Fog of war
Hiding the parts of the map the players have not discovered. yes: built in, revealed as tokens move or by vision. partial: masks the GM paints or reveals by hand, or through a third-party add-on.
Dynamic lighting
Light sources and walls that decide what each player's character can see, worked out by the software as tokens move. partial: light sources or lighting effects without that per-character vision.
Map building
Making maps inside the product rather than only importing a picture made elsewhere. yes: a builder meant for making maps (tiles, assets, terrain or a generator). partial: drawing tools, or placing images on the map.
Levels / height
Verticality: several floors of a building, stairs, flying or elevation, seen as such rather than as separate flat maps.
Sheets & rules
Character sheets and rules automation: the software knows the game's rules (attacks, damage, conditions) instead of only showing tokens. yes: sheets with automation. partial: sheets without automation, or some automation. Trackers alone (hit points, initiative, condition markers) are noted but count as no.
D&D Beyond / Beyond20
Using D&D Beyond characters at the table. yes: an official integration by the VTT's maker or by D&D Beyond. partial: through a third-party bridge, such as Beyond20 (a free browser extension that sends D&D Beyond rolls to a VTT), or a one-way import.
Dice
3D: physical 3D dice that tumble on screen. roller: a dice roller (text or 2D, or rolling whose look isn't stated). no: bring your own. 3D dice from a third-party add-on are in the note.
Phones
Whether a player can take part from a phone. partial: it opens, but some parts do not work or are hard to use.
Offline / self-host
Playing without the maker's servers: offline, on a local network, or on a server you run yourself.
Open source
Whether the source code is published under an open licence (named in the detail view). partial: some core parts only. Source published under a non-commercial licence, or open source promised but not yet published, counts as no (said in the note).
AI features
AI features inside the product: generating maps, art or text, an AI assistant at the table, or machine-learning features such as automatic room or wall detection. This says only that they exist, not whether that is good. How a product was made (AI art, AI-written code) is in its note and summary, not this value.
Mods / API
Extending it. yes: add-ons, modules or scripting that add features. partial: sharing content (such as a workshop), or an API that drives the table without adding features.
Stage
How finished the maker says it is.
Community signal
What users visibly say or how big the visible community is, in one line. Not a score.
Site
Whether the product's own website answered when we last checked: up, gone (the page or the name has disappeared), or ? (it did not say, or there is no website). Hover or open the row for the date.
Group
How the row was made, not how good or how old a product is. Established: well-known products and close competitors, checked against their official sites and docs. New & indie: found through their makers' posts on r/VTT and described from those posts and their sites. Ours: Turnip Works, which we make.
n/a
Not applicable: the feature does not fit that kind of tool (a map maker has no dice).
(?)
A value we could not confirm from a source. Treat it as a lead, not a fact.

Claim yours / correct me

If you make one of these products, or something here is wrong, missing or out of date, tell us and we'll fix the row. Makers: a short note on what's built, with a link to where a reader can see it, is all we need. Products that aren't listed yet are welcome too.

What you send comes privately to us (it is not posted anywhere) and is used to correct the table: the words, the product and anything you type in the last box. Details in the Privacy Policy. You can also email hello@turnipworks.net.

The data

  • turnipworks.net/vtt-index.md: the same as plain text (Markdown).
  • turnipworks.net/vtt-index.schema.json: the JSON Schema the data follows.
