The rosters largely avoid overt caricature. The biggest risks are **mixing centuries and regions**, and letting familiar fantasy silhouettes override documented forms. These are design risks, not claims that everyone in a living tradition will react alike.

### 1. Norse steading

- **Dating:** Urnes ornament dates roughly from 1050 onward—mostly outside this pack. Use Borre/Jelling/Mammen for earlier settings; reserve Ringerike for approximately 1000–1050. [Urnes dating](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00766097.2021.2000250)
- **Architecture:** Tar-black stave walls, carved portals and steep shingles together risk resembling later stave churches. Ground the steading in an excavated regional house: earthfast posts/planks or wattle-and-daub, with an appropriate roof. Shingles are not inherently wrong. [National Museum of Denmark](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/the-people/viking-homes/)
- **Windows:** Windowless ordinary houses are reasonable; “halls had no windows” is too absolute. Viking-age window glass is documented, probably from elite buildings. [Museum research announcement](https://via.ritzau.dk/pressemeddelelse/13731483/the-vikings-had-glass-in-their-windows?lang=en&publisherId=13560791)
- **Overconfidence:** The exact high-seat placement and paired carved pillars should be labeled a reconstruction, not a universal hall arrangement. Likewise, striped sails and continuous shield rows are familiar reconstruction conventions.
- **Sensitivity:** Runes are writing, not meaningless decoration. Use a checked Younger Futhark inscription; distinguish historical symbols from modern extremist uses rather than implying Norse religious imagery is inherently extremist.
- **Missing signature forms:** Add **paired roof-support posts** and a **partitioned cattle byre** for an appropriate farm longhouse. Both communicate its structure and agricultural purpose from above.

### 2. Medieval Middle East

- **Scope/trope:** Rename the visible pack “Mamluk Egypt and Syria.” “Middle East” plus spices, carpets, brass and divans risks a timeless exotic bazaar. Include ordinary workshop/storage furnishings, and keep Cairo and Syrian architectural variants distinct.
- **Qa’a correction:** A generic throne-like dais at one end misses the defining plan: a **lower central durqa’a between raised iwans**, often with a central fountain. This is also the strongest missing tabletop silhouette. [MIT architectural history](https://web.mit.edu/4.615/www/printouts/handout12.html)
- **Hammam correction:** The central octagonal hot marble slab reads strongly as an Ottoman Turkish bath. For medieval Syria, model a documented bath’s sequence of changing, warm and hot spaces, with bathing recesses and basins; do not make that slab universal. [Syrian Heritage Archive](https://syrian-heritage.org/the-public-hammam-an-ancient-syrian-tradition/)
- **Coffee dating/form:** Keep coffeehouses explicitly at the late end of the range. The familiar dallah silhouette needs a dated reference: one museum traces its particular type only to seventeenth-century Baghdad. Use period-supported boiling/serving vessels rather than a modern Gulf coffee set. [Museum With No Frontiers](https://sharinghistory.museumwnf.org/database_item.php?id=object%3BAWE%3Bua%3B32%3Ben)
- **Wall treatment:** Ablaq, tile dado and mashrabiya are not a compulsory combined kit. Use plain plaster for ordinary rooms; reserve richer stone, marble or tile treatments for a documented building and status.
- **Garden caution:** Four axial beds, rills and cypresses suggest a Persianate *chahar bagh*. That is not automatically impossible, but should not stand in for every Cairene or Syrian courtyard.
- **Second missing signature:** Add a **vaulted khan arcade with repeated storeroom entrances** around the caravan courtyard. Its rhythm reads more specifically from above than additional spice sacks.

### 3. Medieval Rus’

- **Identity:** Replace “darker and Russian-built” with actual construction features. Rus’ is not interchangeable with the modern Russian nation; Novgorod, Vladimir-Suzdal and Muscovy also need regional/date labels.
- **Domestic architecture:** Elaborate carved window frames, painted chests and a pristine monumental stove can collectively read as a much later folkloric izba. Prefer restrained decoration and an archaeological house reference.
- **Stove correction:** For earlier ordinary houses, use a simple clay oven with period-appropriate smoke arrangements. Whitewashing itself is not the error; importing the complete later chimneyed “Russian stove” form is.
- **Tiled stove:** Restrict it to a documented late-sixteenth-century elite variant. Avoid the familiar elaborate polychrome seventeenth–eighteenth-century stove. [Museum tile chronology](https://kuzn-krepost.ru/visitors/our-news/russkie-pechnye-izraztsy-kontsa-xvi-1-j-poloviny-xviii-vv-virtualnaya-vystavka-s-30-avgusta-2022-goda.html)
- **Church dating:** A tall, multi-tier iconostasis suits the later medieval end; it should not be the default for an eleventh-century church. Provide an earlier, simpler sanctuary-screen variant.
- **Religious sensitivity:** “Generic saints’ panels” can produce invented or incoherent sacred imagery. Use documented icon compositions respectfully; place the sanctuary and altar **behind** the screen, rather than treating the iconostasis as wallpaper.
- **Missing signature forms:** Add **timber-paved streets** and **fenced courtyard compounds with gates**. These give medieval Novgorod a distinctive overhead plan, supported by excavation. [Russian Academy of Sciences archaeology](https://www.ra.iaran.ru/?lang=ru&page_id=1235)

### 4. Late Postclassic Mexica

- **Identity/sensitivity:** Title this “Mexica: Tenochtitlan and Tlatelolco,” rather than implying it represents all Mesoamerica. Nahua peoples are living communities; avoid presenting their culture solely as vanished temple scenery.
- **Chacmool:** **Keep it—it is not exclusively Maya or Toltec.** A painted example stood before the Tlaloc shrine at Templo Mayor. Make it shrine-specific rather than a standard entrance furnishing everywhere. [INAH archaeological description](https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/es/pagina-de-elemento/1261)
- **Sacred framing:** Omitting gore is a sound asset choice. Avoid implying that a maize-and-flower-only shrine exhausts Mexica ritual practice, or that historical sacrificial objects are inherently colonial inventions.
- **Ballcourt correction:** Specify an **I-shaped court with transverse end zones**, not merely two sloping walls. Ring placement and wall profiles need a central-Mexican reference; avoid accidentally copying Chichén Itzá.
- **Housing:** “Commoners’ huts” understates an urban society. Include modest rectangular houses grouped around courtyards, with separate cooking/storage areas; reserve thatched field shelters for appropriate settings.
- **Architectural motif:** Stepped frets are plausible, but not a universal Mexica wall treatment. Use a documented local pattern and placement rather than an all-purpose “Mesoamerican” trim.
- **Scribe’s room:** Screenfold manuscripts are appropriate; the low writing table is less securely diagnostic. A seated-on-mat working arrangement is a safer reference-led option. Paint actual pictorial conventions, not invented “Aztec glyphs.”
- **Missing signature forms:** Add the **paired summit shrines and twin stairways of Templo Mayor**, and a **small canal landing/crossing module**. These identify the Mexica ceremonial and lake-city landscape far better than another generic pyramid or pot.
