# Culture packs B: rosters (2026-11-06)

Four small culture packs for Turnip Works (a browser 3D tabletop; house style "heroic hand-painted" miniatures, seen
from a table camera above). Each pack = a wall family (walls, doorway, window) + a roof style from the existing set + a
few signature room types + ~15-22 new props; generic props (beds, barrels, tables, rugs) are reused. Aim: real period
forms, respectful, no caricature or costume props; living religions' sacred spaces with dignity; Mesoamerica without
the "bloodthirsty savage" trope.

## 1. Norse steading (code NRS): Viking-Age Scandinavia, c. 800-1050
- Walls `norse-stave`: upright split-log staves, dark tarred timber, a sill beam and a wall plate with ONE band of
  carved interlace (Urnes/Ringerike-style beasts); doorway a low heavy timber portal with carved jambs. No windows (halls
  were lit by the hearth, the smoke hole and the doors). Roof: wood shingle, steep.
- Rooms: Longhouse (long hearth down the middle, raised sleeping platforms with fleeces along both long walls, the
  warp-weighted loom by the door end, cauldron on its crane, sea chests); Mead hall (the long hearth, the chieftain's
  HIGH SEAT in the middle of a long wall between its two carved high-seat pillars, trestle tables and benches, round
  shields hung in rows); Norse smithy (low stone forge with a stone bellows-shield, anvil on a stump, quench trough);
  Boathouse/naust (a small clinker-built boat on its stocks, oars, the furled striped sail on trestles); Steading yard
  (outdoor: a painted runestone with a serpent band of runes, well, woodpile, a fish-drying rack); Weaving hut
  (pit-house: the loom, baskets of wool and spindles, a small hearth).
- New props: long hearth, sleeping platform, high seat, warp-weighted loom, sea chest, shield row, runestone, small boat
  on stocks, oar rack, furled sail on trestles, fish-drying rack, Norse forge, wool baskets, rotary quern, spear rack.
- Deliberately NOT: horned helmets, skull cups, "blood eagle", berserker props, Nazi-appropriated symbols (no
  valknut-as-logo, no swastika, no "black sun"); runes are a decorative serpent band, not a slogan.

## 2. Middle East (code MDE): medieval Islamic Near East, Mamluk Cairo / Damascus / Aleppo, 13th-16th c.
- Walls `mde-plaster`: cream lime plaster over stone; the lower courses ABLAQ (alternating dark and honey stone bands)
  with a geometric tile dado band; doorway a pointed arch with striped voussoirs; window a MASHRABIYA (turned-wood lattice
  screen). Roof: flat, whitewashed (existing `plaster`).
- Rooms: Caravanserai courtyard (outdoor: a well or fountain, water trough, corded bales of cloth, saddle packs, big
  water jars, hitching posts; the caravans' animals are figures, never props); Souk shop (a trade of the market's shop
  skeleton: the merchant's raised platform-counter, niche shelves of brass and copper wares, heaped open spice sacks,
  rolled carpets); Hammam (the octagonal heated marble platform in the middle, marble wall basins with brass bowls,
  benches); Audience hall/qa'a (a raised carpeted dais with bolsters at the far end, built-in divan seating along the
  side walls, a floor fountain in the middle, pierced brass lanterns); Coffee house (16th c.: divans, low brass tray
  tables with cups, the coffee hearth: a brazier with long-spouted dallah pots); Courtyard garden (outdoor: four beds
  cut by stone rills, a central pool/fountain, citrus in tubs, cypress trees).
- New props: hammam platform, wall basin, divan section, low brass tray table, coffee brazier with pots, carpeted dais,
  bolster cushions, corded bales, saddle packs, tall water jar, merchant's platform counter, niche shelf of brass wares,
  heaped spice sacks, rolled-carpet stack, cypress tree, stone rill section, inlaid chest.
- Deliberately NOT: a mosque or Qur'an props (no sacred text as decoration), no hookah/"harem"/belly-dancer/flying-carpet
  Orientalism, no scimitars-as-decor, no Arabic-looking pseudo-script (any inscription is abstract geometric pattern).

## 3. Medieval Rus' (code RUS): Novgorod / Vladimir-Suzdal / Muscovy, 11th-16th c.
- Walls `rus-log`: horizontal round pine logs with notched corners (the library's log cabin, darker and Russian-built),
  a carved board under the wall plate; doorway with a carved lintel board; a small window with a carved frame and mica
  panes. Roof: wood shingle.
- Rooms: Izba (the great whitewashed clay stove in the corner by the door, the "beautiful corner" diagonal from it: an
  icon shelf with a lamp over the table, built-in benches along the walls, a painted chest, a distaff); Terem hall (a
  boyar's upper chamber: a tiled stove, a carved high-backed chair, painted chests, a long table with an embroidered
  cloth); Banya (the stone heater, the tiered steaming shelf, tubs and ladles, birch besoms on pegs); Wooden church
  (the ICONOSTASIS across the far wall with its central doors, candle stands, a lectern with an icon; no pews, as in
  Orthodox use); Market row (a trade of the shop skeleton: furs, wax and honey, birch-bark boxes); Stockade yard
  (outdoor: a pointed-log palisade, the well-sweep, a sledge, haystack and woodpile).
- New props: Russian stove, icon corner shelf, tiled stove, carved high chair, painted chest, iconostasis section,
  lectern with icon, banya heater, steaming shelf, birch-besom rack, sledge, well-sweep, palisade section, fur bundles,
  stall counter, shelf of wax/honey/birch-bark wares.
- Deliberately NOT: no bears-with-balalaikas, no samovars (18th c.), no matryoshka (19th c.), no fur-hat-and-vodka
  gags; icons are painted with dignity (generic saints' panels, no caricature), the church is a place of worship.

## 4. Mesoamerica (code MES): Late Postclassic Nahua (Mexica Tenochtitlan / Tlatelolco), c. 1400-1520
- Walls `mes-stucco`: lime-plastered stone, cream-white, a red-painted base band and a frieze of stepped fret
  (xicalcoliuhqui); doorways are open (a heavy lintel, a hanging cotton curtain, no hinged door). No windows. Roof: flat
  whitewashed plaster on stone buildings; thatch on the chinampa and commoners' huts.
- Rooms: Temple summit shrine (the shrine room on top of a pyramid: a stone altar with offerings of maize, flowers and
  copal incense, two great painted ceramic braziers, a chacmool at the entrance, reed mats; NOT gore: no sacrificial
  blood, no skull racks); Ballcourt (outdoor: the two long sloping stone benches with the stone rings at mid-court, end
  markers); Noble's house (a pilli's house: reed mats, the backed reed seat (icpalli), reed chests, a hearth of three
  stones with a clay griddle (comal), the grinding stone (metate), water jars); Market plaza (outdoor, Tlatelolco: vendors'
  spreads in ROWS on mats: maize, chilies and beans, pottery, cotton cloth, cacao; cloth shade awnings); Chinampa garden
  (outdoor: long raised beds of maize between canals, willow trees at their edges, a dugout canoe); Scribe's room
  (tlacuilo: a screenfold codex painted and unfolded on a low table, paint pots, bark-paper stacks, a codex chest).
- New props: chacmool, altar with offerings, great brazier, ballcourt wall with ring, ballcourt wall, icpalli seat, reed
  chest, metate and mano, three-stone hearth with comal, painted water jar, five vendor spreads, shade awning, chinampa
  maize bed, dugout canoe, ahuejote willow, codex on its low table, paint-pot set.
- Deliberately NOT: no sacrificial-stone-with-blood, no tzompantli skull rack, no obsidian-knife-in-heart props, no
  feathered-headdress-as-costume prop, no "Aztec calendar stone as decor" cliché; the shrine is shown as a place of
  offering and worship.
