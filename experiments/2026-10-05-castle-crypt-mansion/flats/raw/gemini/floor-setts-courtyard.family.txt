### Sheet 1: floor-setts-courtyard-family-rot.jpg

1.  **Continuity:** It reads as a single, continuous floor, but one with a very chaotic and irregular pattern. It looks like an old, somewhat haphazardly laid cobblestone or sett-paved courtyard where sections may have been repaired or laid by different workers at different times.

2.  **Grid Visibility:** **Score: 4 (Obvious)**. The grid is highly visible, not as hard lines, but as unnatural breaks in the paving pattern. The primary giveaway is the **broken joints** at the cell edges. Rows of stones abruptly change orientation by 90 degrees, creating jarring seams where horizontal stones meet the ends of vertical stones. This is something that would not happen in real-world masonry and immediately reveals the underlying grid structure.

3.  **Material Consistency:** **Yes**. The four variants look like they are made of the exact same material. They share a consistent color palette (greys and sandy browns), the same level of contrast, and an identical scale and style for the stone textures and weathering. The variation feels like it comes from the same quarry.

### Sheet 2: floor-setts-courtyard-family-straight.jpg

1.  **Continuity:** Yes, it reads perfectly as one continuous floor. It depicts a weathered cobblestone or sett pavement laid in a consistent, horizontal running bond pattern, typical of a street or courtyard.

2.  **Grid Visibility:** **Score: 1 (Very Subtle)**. The grid is almost invisible. The horizontal mortar lines are continuous across the image, perfectly hiding the horizontal cell boundaries. The vertical joints are staggered effectively, so there are no obvious vertical seams. The only potential clue is a very subtle repetition of specific stone shapes or color clusters if you stare long enough, but in normal viewing, the grid is not apparent.

3.  **Material Consistency:** **Yes**. The variants blend seamlessly and appear to be the same material. The color, contrast, and texture scale are uniform across the entire sheet. The subtle differences between the cells successfully mimic the natural, non-repeating look of a real stone floor.
