### Sheet: floor-marble-cabochon-family-rot.jpg

1.  **Does it read as ONE continuous floor? What floor?**
    Yes, this reads very effectively as a single, continuous floor. It appears to be a classic marble tile floor with a "cabochon" or "dot" pattern, where larger, light-colored marble tiles are accented by smaller, dark square tiles at their corners. The random rotation of the cells helps sell the illusion of a varied, natural installation.

2.  **How visible is the cell grid? Score and explanation.**
    **Score: 2/5 (Subtle)**
    The grid is not immediately obvious, but it can be found upon inspection. The primary giveaway is the **cut-off shapes** of the marble veins. A distinct vein will run to the edge of a 5-ft cell and stop abruptly, failing to continue into the adjacent cell. There are no hard lines or significant tone steps, but these broken texture joints reveal the underlying grid.

3.  **Do the four variants look like the same material?**
    Yes, absolutely. The variants are very consistent in their material properties. They share the same base color, the same color palette for the veining, a consistent level of contrast, and the same texture scale. They convincingly look like different cuts from the same block of marble.

### Sheet: floor-marble-cabochon-family-straight.jpg

1.  **Does it read as ONE continuous floor? What floor?**
    It reads as a continuous *patterned* floor, but the illusion of it being one single, natural installation is weaker than the rotated version. It is the same marble cabochon tile floor, but the lack of rotation makes the repetition of the texture variants much more noticeable, giving it a more artificial, "tiled" look.

2.  **How visible is the cell grid? Score and explanation.**
    **Score: 4/5 (Obvious)**
    The cell grid is much more visible here. In addition to the **cut-off shapes** of marble veins at the cell edges (the same issue as the "-rot" sheet), the lack of rotation creates a strong sense of repetition. The eye can easily spot identical cells across the floor, which immediately reveals the grid structure and the limited number of texture variants. This repeating pattern is the most significant giveaway.

3.  **Do the four variants look like the same material?**
    Yes. As with the rotated sheet, the material properties (colour, contrast, scale) are perfectly consistent across all variants. They look like they are made from the exact same material.
