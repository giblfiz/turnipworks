### **Sheet: floor-tomb-slabs-family-rot.jpg**

1.  **Continuity & Floor Type:** Yes, it reads as one continuous floor. It looks like a well-worn, slightly uneven stone paver floor from a tomb, crypt, or ancient hall, featuring various inset memorial slabs. The random orientation of the slabs enhances the natural, aged look.

2.  **Grid Visibility:** **1/5 (Almost invisible)**. The grid is extremely well-hidden. The random rotation is highly effective at breaking up any continuous lines that would give away the cell edges. While subtle tonal shifts between adjacent cells exist if you look closely, the mismatched orientations of the pavers at the seams create a chaotic, organic pattern that successfully masks the underlying grid structure.

3.  **Variant Consistency:** Yes, the four variants look like the same material. They share a consistent dark grey color palette, lighting, contrast, and texture scale for the stone pavers. The style of the carved symbols on the inset slabs is also uniform.

### **Sheet: floor-tomb-slabs-family-straight.jpg**

1.  **Continuity & Floor Type:** It mostly reads as a single floor type—a dark stone paver floor with memorial slabs, suitable for a tomb. However, the repetition and alignment issues make it feel more like a manufactured, tiled surface rather than a naturally laid stone floor.

2.  **Grid Visibility:** **4/5 (Very obvious)**. The grid is highly visible. The primary giveaway is the **broken joints**; the horizontal mortar lines of the pavers run perfectly straight within a cell but stop abruptly at the vertical cell boundaries, creating strong, repeating vertical seams. The same effect happens at the horizontal boundaries. The perfectly aligned, grid-based placement of the decorative slabs further reinforces the cell structure.

3.  **Variant Consistency:** Yes, the variants look like the same material. They all use the same consistent color, contrast, and texture scale, which ironically makes the grid seams created by the non-continuous paver lines even more noticeable.
