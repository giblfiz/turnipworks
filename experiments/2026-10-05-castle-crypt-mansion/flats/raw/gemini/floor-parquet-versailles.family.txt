### **Sheet: floor-parquet-versailles-family-rot.jpg**

1.  **Continuity & Floor Type:**
    Yes, it reads as one continuous floor. The pattern is a classic wood parquet, specifically the **Parquet de Versailles** style, characterized by interlaced diagonal strips within a square wooden frame. The random rotations give it the appearance of a meticulously hand-laid floor where individual panels are oriented differently for artistic effect.

2.  **Grid Visibility: 4/5 (Obvious)**
    The 4x4 cell grid is quite visible. It's given away by two main factors:
    *   **Frame Doubling:** Each parquet tile has its own border. Where two cells meet, their borders are placed side-by-side, creating a "double-thick" frame line that is noticeably thicker than the internal framing of the pattern.
    *   **Broken Joints:** The random rotations cause the wood grain and the direction of the frame pieces to change abruptly at cell edges. A horizontal frame piece will suddenly meet a vertical one, creating a harsh, unnatural joint that clearly delineates the cell boundary.

3.  **Variant Consistency:**
    Yes, the four variants look like they are made from the exact same material. The color palette, wood grain texture, contrast levels, and the scale of the wooden strips are perfectly consistent across all cells, regardless of their rotation.

### **Sheet: floor-parquet-versailles-family-straight.jpg**

1.  **Continuity & Floor Type:**
    Yes, this also reads as a single continuous floor. It is the same **Parquet de Versailles** pattern. The uniform orientation creates a very orderly, grid-like appearance that is common for this type of high-end flooring. It looks like a single, large, unified surface.

2.  **Grid Visibility: 3/5 (Noticeable)**
    The cell grid is still noticeable, but less jarringly so than in the rotated version.
    *   **Frame Doubling:** The primary giveaway is still the double-thick framing where cells meet. This creates a clear grid pattern across the floor.
    *   **Cut-off Shapes:** The diagonal, interwoven pattern inside the frame is abruptly cut off at the cell edge and does not continue into the next cell, breaking the illusion of a seamless weave.
    *   The grid is less obvious than the "-rot" version because the outer frames are all aligned, so the wood grain direction is continuous across the entire floor, which helps hide the seams slightly.

3.  **Variant Consistency:**
    Yes, the variants are perfectly consistent. They share an identical color scheme, contrast, and scale. They convincingly look like panels cut from the same batch of wood and finished in the same style.
