#### e) Honest verdict: Cheap Game Effect vs. Painted-Miniature-World Effect
* **Verdict**: **Cheap game effect** (looks like standard MMO/VTT additive billboard/cylinder VFX dropped onto 3D models).
* **What makes it look cheap**:
  1. **Glowing fog in front of the figure**: Real or painted miniatures never have milky translucent haze floating in empty air in front of their faces, which flattens the miniature's painted shading.
  2. **Neon UI-style base rings**: The uniform, over-bright ring hugging the base perimeter looks like an RTS selection circle rather than physical light hitting a painted base and tabletop.
  3. **Floating billboard columns and ghosted decal**: The tall cylindrical shafts on L4/L5 and the semi-transparent 2D sun graphic hanging in mid-air break tabletop scale and materiality.
* **Three most important changes toward a painted-miniature (OSL) look**:
  1. **Clear the front of the miniature and push the effect into surface lighting (true OSL)**: Remove or strongly stencil out the aura haze in front of the pawn so the miniature's face and paint job remain crisp; instead, drive intensity via strong directional rim/under-lighting on the miniature's mesh normals and radial light spill across the grey base and wooden floor planks.
  2. **Put vertical aura geometry strictly *behind* the figure (or sculpt it like painted translucent resin/flame)**: Render the rising glow behind the pawn's silhouette so the miniature stands in high-contrast relief against a warm backdrop rather than inside a milky tube.
  3. **Replace the neon base ring and ghostly sun texture with physical/heraldic equivalents**: Soften the base ring into grazed light across the bevel of the grey base + floor wood grain, and render the L5 emblem as a crisp, high-contrast heraldic crest/halo behind the figure's head (unaffected by background wall seams or darkvision fog).
