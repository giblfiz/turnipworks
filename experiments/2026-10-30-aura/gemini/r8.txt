#### e) Honest Verdict: Cheap Game Effect vs. Painted-Miniature-World Effect
* **Verdict:** **CHEAP GAME EFFECT.**
* **What makes it look cheap:**
  1. **Translucent additive shells drawn *in front of* the mini instead of lighting its surfaces:** Painted OSL (Object Source Lighting) works by accentuating the 3D sculpt's raised edges while leaving crevices dark. Here, semi-transparent glowing cards sit between the camera and the miniature, flattening contrast, blowing out `L4`, and showing crossed-billboard seams.
  2. **Tall mid-air laser columns (`L4`, `L5`), floating spark dots, and a floating 2D decal:** Vertical columns of fire hanging in empty air and floating 2D sun stickers are classic MMO spell billboards, incompatible with physical tabletop miniatures.
  3. **Additive blending over a bright orange floor:** The warm floorboards contaminate the additive shaders (`silver` turning orange, `L1`/`L2` disappearing into the wood).
* **Three most important changes to move toward a painted-miniature look:**
  1. **Shift from volumetric air shells to surface-normal OSL on the miniature and base:** Apply directional rim/point lighting directly to the miniature's mesh normals and base texture (bright highlights on raised armor/folds, deep shadows in recesses) and clamp any foreground haze so the miniature's face and paint job remain crisp (`L4`).
  2. **Eliminate crossed billboard cards and tall empty-air tubes:** Remove the intersecting planar cards causing seams on `silver`/`blue`/`red`. Keep any physical flame/magic geometry sculpted and behind/around the base rim (like translucent resin tabletop effects), and express high levels (`L4`–`L5`) via wider, richer light cast onto the surrounding floorboards/walls rather than tall vertical sky-tubes.
  3. **Ground the `L5` emblem and fix `silver`'s colour blending:** Render the `L5` emblem either as a crisp light projection on the floor/base around the pawn or as a distinct sculpted halo/banner crest behind the mini's head, and switch `silver` to alpha/premultiplied blending (or cool silver-blue shadows with crisp white highlights) so it never turns orange over the wood floor.
