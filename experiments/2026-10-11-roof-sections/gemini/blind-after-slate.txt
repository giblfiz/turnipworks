### Roof Description

1.  **Material:** The roofs are covered with what appears to be dark grey slate tiles or possibly wooden shingles. I am highly confident it is one of these two materials, based on the rectangular shape, color, and overlapping pattern.

2.  **Relief:** The roof surface reads as a flat picture (a texture) laid on a simple plane. The edges at the eaves and gables are perfectly sharp and have no visible thickness, which would not be the case with real, overlapping shingles. There is no self-shadowing between the rows of shingles that would indicate physical relief.

3.  **Continuity:** The rows of roofing are not continuous or seamless at junctions.
    *   **Valley:** In the valley of Building 1, where the two roof sections meet, the textures are cut off abruptly and do not align, creating a harsh, jagged seam.
    *   **Ridge:** The ridges on both buildings are simple sharp edges where the planes meet, with no ridge cap tiles.
    *   **Chimneys:** The roof texture stops dead against the base of the chimneys with no flashing.

4.  **Style:** The roof texture fits the look of the walls and chimneys. Both the roof and the rest of the building share a chunky, somewhat stylized, hand-painted appearance, rather than being photorealistic. They look like they belong together.

5.  **Broken or Wrong Elements:**
    *   There is a large, jagged black hole in the roof of the front-facing section of Building 1.
    *   The front gable of Building 2 has a large triangular section that is a flat, untextured tan color, as if it is unfinished.
    *   The texture seam in the valley of Building 1 is wrong; the shingle patterns clip into each other instead of meeting properly.
    *   The rows of shingles on the two sections of Building 1 are not horizontally aligned with each other.
