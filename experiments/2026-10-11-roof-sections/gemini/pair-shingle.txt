### 1. Description of Roof Surfaces

*   **Image A:** The roof surface is made of individually modeled, three-dimensional tiles. This is evident from the distinct shadows each tile casts on the one below it, creating a textured surface with physical relief. The light catches the top edges of the tiles, and the gaps between them are dark, indicating real depth. The material appears to be weathered clay or slate tiles.

*   **Image B:** The roof surface is a flat texture applied to the simple geometric planes of the roof. It is a 2D picture of tiles, not a 3D model of them. This is clear because there is no self-shadowing between individual tiles; the "shadows" and "highlights" are painted into the texture and do not change with the angle of the surface. The entire roof plane is lit uniformly, lacking the fine-grained light and shadow play seen in A.

### 2. Physical Model vs. Cheaper Appearance

*   **Image A** looks more like a physical model. The three-dimensional relief of the tiles gives it a tangible, miniature-like quality. It has depth and texture that you could imagine feeling with your fingers.
*   **Image B** looks cheaper. The flat, painted-on texture is a common technique to save on performance and modeling time. It lacks the depth and realism of A, making it look more like a simple video game asset than a high-quality digital miniature. The lack of physical interaction with light makes it appear less convincing.
