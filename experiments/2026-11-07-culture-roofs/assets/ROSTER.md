# Culture roofs roster (Turnip Works, 2026-11-07): roofs and roof ornaments for seven culture packs

A browser 3D tabletop game. Rooms of each culture pack get a roof (seen from above, at table distance) and a few
generated roof ornaments. House style: heroic hand-painted. Nothing carries text or script (generated script is
gibberish), and nothing on a sacred building carries figures or deity images. The question is what is right, respectful
and period-anchored for each.

## Roofing surfaces (painted tile pictures plus a 3D course section, tiled over every slope)
- **kawara** (Japan, Kyoto, late Edo, c. 1780-1860): silver-grey interlocking clay pan tiles (sangawara) on townhouses
  (machiya), shops and a dojo. Roof form: irimoya (hip-and-gable) with a gentle upward eave curve. Ridge: stacked
  noshi-gawara under a round cap tile. Ridge ends: an onigawara ridge-end tile, face TBD (an oni demon face vs a plain
  or wave/cloud relief).
- **Shinto shrine hall** (same Kyoto late-Edo setting): keeps a cypress-bark-like wood shingle (hiwadabuki-like) with the
  same irimoya form and eave curve. Question: should the shrine hall carry chigi (crossed forked finials at the gable
  ends) and katsuogi (short logs across the ridge)? The game will NOT add them unless they fit a Kyoto late-Edo shrine.
- **tongwa** (China, Beijing, mid-Qing, c. 1730-1800): grey cylinder tiles (concave pan tiles under round cover tiles in
  straight columns) on ordinary courtyard houses (siheyuan) of grey brick. Ridge: grey stacked tiles.
- **glazed** (same Beijing): the Buddhist temple hall's glazed tile roof. Question: green, yellow, or grey with green
  glazed edging? (yellow glaze was reserved for imperial buildings). Ridge: glazed ridge to match. Ridge ends: a chiwen
  (the dragon-fish acroterion, mouth biting the ridge, tail curling up), glazed.
- **sod** (Norse, Viking Age c. 800-1050): pitched turf (grass over birch bark) on longhouses and halls, ragged, no joints.
- **tes** (Rus', Novgorod / Vladimir-Suzdal / Muscovy, 11th-16th c.): plank roof of long split boards running down the
  slope, on log houses (izba), a terem, a banya and market stalls.

## Roof ornaments (generated 3D meshes, placed by rule)
- **roof-cupola-onion** (Rus'): a wooden church's cupola: a tall drum and an onion dome of aspen ploughshare shingles
  (lemekh), small finial, on the ridge of a wooden church room. Questions: a cross on top or not? For 11th-16th c.
  Novgorod/Muscovy is an onion dome right, or should it be a helmet dome or a tent (shatyor) roof?
- **roof-dome** (Mamluk Cairo/Damascus, 13th-16th c.): a stone/plaster dome on a low drum over a hammam, with small round
  glass oculi (light holes) in the dome.
- **roof-malqaf** (Mamluk Cairo): a wind-catcher, a slanted timber hood open to the north, on the roof of a qa'a
  (reception hall).
- **ind-chhatri** (Rajput, Jaipur/Amber, 18th c.; already exists in the game): small domed pavilions on the corners of
  haveli and palace flat roofs.
- **roof-shikhara** (north India, Nagara style): a curvilinear sandstone temple tower over the sanctum of a Shiva temple,
  with an amalaka (ribbed stone disc) and a kalasha (pot finial) on top; no figures, no deity images, no text.
- **roof-ridge --kawara / --tongwa / --glazed**: 5-ft ridge sections for the three tile roofs above.
- **Norse halls**: no ornament planned (no dragon-head gable finials, which belong to later stave churches?).
