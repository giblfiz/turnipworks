The main risks are mixing periods, turning ordinary buildings into monuments, and treating “no sacred figures” as a historical religious rule. That restriction is an art-direction choice, and it conflicts with the proposed chiwen.

## kawara

- **Choose plain or cloud-relief ridge ends.** Oni-faced tiles are authentic protective imagery, not inherently offensive; avoid presenting them as evidence of “demon worship.” [JAANUS: onigawara](https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/o/onigawara.htm).
- Sangawara fits late Edo, but each tile combines concave and convex portions; it is not a separate pan-and-cover system or necessarily a modern mechanically interlocking tile. [JAANUS: sangawarabuki](https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/s/sangawarabuki.htm).
- **Use simple kirizuma gables for ordinary machiya and shops.** Universal irimoya and conspicuous upturned corners would over-monumentalize Kyoto; reserve irimoya for selected larger buildings.
- **From above:** add a lower street-front *hisashi* roof and a restrained, straight eave edge. These convey machiya better than exaggerated curvature. [Kyoto City](https://kyoto-bunkaisan.city.kyoto.lg.jp/kyotoisan/nintei-theme/kyoumachiya.html).

## Shinto shrine hall

- **No chigi or katsuogi for this unspecified irimoya hall.** They are legitimate shrine features, but depend on the building tradition; they are not universal Shinto identifiers. [Kokugakuin University](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/id=9688).
- Irimoya is appropriate for a worship/ceremonial hall: Kamigamo’s Hosodono and Hashidono existed in this form before late Edo. Do not confuse these with its *nagare-zukuri* main sanctuary. [Kamigamo Shrine](https://www.kamigamojinja.jp/en/grounds/).
- **Correct the material:** *hiwadabuki* is layered cypress bark; split wooden shingles are *kokerabuki*. “Bark-like wood shingles” conflates distinct techniques. [Japanese conservation institute](https://tobunken.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/9082/files/2016sentei.pdf).
- **From above:** show closely layered, thick bark eaves and a substantial ridge covering; avoid ordinary plank-shingle joints.

## tongwa

- Grey pan-and-cover roofing is credible. Strictly, *tongwa* are the convex cylindrical covers; *banwa* are the concave pans underneath. Keep this visibly different from sangawara. [Peking University](https://news.pku.edu.cn/info/2881/2730261.htm).
- No intrinsic sensitivity concern. Avoid miniature-palace treatment: ordinary courtyard houses should not acquire enormous curled eaves, imperial beasts or elaborate ceremonial ridges.
- Use restrained gabled forms, especially **yingshan**, with gable walls meeting the roof edges. The ridge should be Chinese brick-and-tile construction, not Japanese noshi courses.
- **From above:** emphasize straight cover-tile ribs and substantial masonry gable borders.

## glazed

- **Choose green.** It is a defensible non-imperial default; Beijing’s Jietai Temple provides a regional green-glazed hall reference, though its present covering does not establish unchanged eighteenth-century fabric. [Beijing tourism authority](https://r.visitbeijing.com.cn/attraction/46).
- “Yellow was reserved for imperial buildings” needs qualification: **imperially sponsored Buddhist temples could also use yellow**, as at Qianlong’s Xihuang Temple. [Beijing government](https://english.beijing.gov.cn/specials/beijingmagazine/list/202410/P020241025353969991532.pdf).
- **Omit chiwen under the literal no-figures rule.** It is a figural guardian creature, although historically appropriate and not intrinsically disrespectful. If architectural guardians become an exception, use paired Qing ridge-swallowing dragon forms, not generic freestanding fish. [Palace Museum](https://www.dpm.org.cn/lemmas/241554.html).
- **From above:** emphasize green ridge bands and regular pan-and-cover relief; use restrained glaze highlights.

## sod

- **Keep, but regionalize:** particularly suitable for North Atlantic Norse settings. Turf roofing should not imply that all Norse buildings were primitive, ragged or identical.
- Birch bark beneath sod is plausible. “No joints” describes the grown surface, not construction: turf was laid in pieces. [Archaeologist Birgitta Wallace on L’Anse aux Meadows](https://canadianmysteries.ca/sites/vinland/lanseauxmeadows/buildings/4564en.html).
- **From above:** show a thick earth edge beneath the vegetation and a small framed smoke opening; avoid a uniformly shaggy carpet or modern masonry chimney.

## tes

- **Keep downslope split-and-dressed boards**, but show overlapping or double coverage rather than open decking seams.
- The six-century, three-region scope is too broad for one exact elite roof. Avoid importing picturesque nineteenth-century fretwork or a fairy-tale “terem” silhouette into every medieval dwelling.
- **From above:** add a heavy timber ridge cover and defined eave troughs/retainers. Traditional examples support these details, but surviving later buildings do not prove every eleventh-century arrangement. [Kizhi Museum roofing exhibition](https://site.kizhi.karelia.ru/info/en/about/pressrelease/2025/15263.html).

## roof-cupola-onion

- **Cross: yes**, plain and without a corpus. It identifies an Orthodox church and does not violate the prohibition on figures or deity images.
- **Choose a modest onion for a specifically late-sixteenth-century variant; reject it as the universal eleventh–sixteenth-century roof.** Early dome chronology is disputed; replacing every onion with a supposedly certain “helmet” would also overclaim. [Lidov’s study](https://hierotopy.ru/contents/LidovSection_1_OnionDomes_IconographyArchitecture_1990_Eng.pdf), [contrasting architectural research](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334079937_Dome_roofs_and_domes_of_orthodox_churches).
- **No interchangeable shatyor ornament:** a tent roof is a structural building composition, sometimes topped by a cupola. The famous 1532 Kolomenskoye example is masonry, not evidence for an eleventh-century wooden church. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/634/).
- Avoid the oversized clustered silhouette of eighteenth-century Kizhi as shorthand for all medieval Rus’.
- **From above:** use overlapping silver-grey aspen scales and a restrained bulb/neck proportion; place it over the principal church volume rather than automatically on every room’s ridge. [Kizhi Museum: aspen shingles](https://site.kizhi.karelia.ru/info/about/fotovyistavka-vozrozhdennyij-shedevr-rossii/13609.html).

## roof-dome

- **Keep the hammam dome and glass lights.** Specify masonry with plaster finish: plaster is not an alternative structural material. Brick domes also occur; exposed stone construction should not be universal. [Archnet’s Mamluk bath report](https://s3.amazonaws.com/media.archnet.org/system/publications/contents/774/original/FLS0784.pdf?1384748665=).
- The apertures should have **glass caps/bowls**, not open holes that release heat. Domes and vaults can vary across the bath’s rooms. [Damascus bath survey](https://www.academia.edu/15348908/Hammam_al_Safi_An_Ayyubid_Bath_in_Damascus_Zeitschrift_fu_r_Orient_Arch%C3%A4ologie_3_2010_142_161).
- Avoid an exotic pleasure-palace stereotype or a monumental mosque/tomb dome. For political dating, Mamluk rule here ends in **1516–17**, although building traditions continue.
- **From above:** use ordered clusters of raised glass eyes and, where the plan permits, several smaller domes or short vaults.

## roof-malqaf

- **Keep the sloping timber scoop.** It is functional Cairene architecture; replacing it with a tall, multi-sided Iranian wind tower would flatten distinct traditions.
- **Northward is an acceptable simplification, not an exact universal bearing.** King’s historical reconstruction identifies NNE-facing examples and the medieval name *bādhahanj*; *malqaf* remains a useful modern asset label. [King’s historical study](https://www.academia.edu/43996180/The_wind_catchers_of_medieval_Cairo_and_their_secrets_1001_years_of_renewable_energy_PART_ONE_OF_TWO).
- **From above:** emphasize the dark intake mouth and closed sloping back. Connect it to the hall’s ventilation shaft rather than placing it as arbitrary decoration.

## ind-chhatri

- **Keep for elite haveli/palace roofs**, with open columns supporting the canopy. Do not scatter them over every ordinary dwelling. Amber supplies an appropriate regional architectural reference. [Rajasthan Tourism](https://www.tourism.rajasthan.gov.in/content/rajasthan-tourism/en/tourist-destinations/amber-palace.html).
- Chhatris belong to overlapping Rajput and Indo-Islamic traditions; avoid claiming a culturally “pure” invention. Rooftop pavilions are also not automatically funerary cenotaphs.
- **From above:** add a broad projecting *chhajja* beneath the dome and a dark open gap around the supports, so it reads as a pavilion rather than a solid turret.

## roof-shikhara

- **Choose latina/rekha-prasada** for the proposed single curvilinear tower. Nagara is a broader family, not one universal silhouette; amalaka and kalasha are appropriate components. [NCERT architectural overview](https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/sathee-clat/student-corner/ncert-books/class-11/an-introduction-to-indian-art/chapter-06-temple-architecture-and-sculpture/).
- **A date and regional prototype are missing.** If this belongs to the Jaipur/Amber pack, anchor it to that setting before claiming historical accuracy; sandstone and “north India” alone are insufficient.
- Figure-free treatment is an artistic simplification, **not a Hindu requirement**. Do not imply that historical sculpted temples are less respectful.
- **From above:** articulate the tower’s projecting vertical ribs and the amalaka’s scalloped outline. Keep the adjoining mandapa roof lower; do not repeat sanctum towers over every room.

## roof-ridge — kawara / tongwa / glazed

- **Use three constructions**, not one recoloured mesh: Japanese noshi stack and cap; modest Chinese brick/tile ridge; moulded glazed Chinese ridge components.
- Five-foot sections are game modules, not historical joint spacing. Hide module seams and put terminal ornaments only at actual ridge ends.
- Avoid “pan-Asian” interchangeability: Japanese oni tiles and Qing ridge-swallowing dragons have different forms and contexts. [JAANUS](https://www.aisf.or.jp/~jaanus/deta/o/onigawara.htm), [Palace Museum](https://www.dpm.org.cn/lemmas/241554.html).
- **From above:** strengthen the distinctive cap profiles and maintain continuous courses across modules.

## Norse halls

- **Keep dragon finials absent by default.** This is a conservative choice that avoids turning every hall into a longship.
- **No, animal roof-end imagery does not belong exclusively to later stave churches.** Viking-age house-shaped hogback monuments carry end-beasts, although these are indirect evidence for actual building ornament—not proof of ubiquitous dragon finials. [Corpus of Anglo-Saxon Stone Sculpture](https://ascorpus.ac.uk/vol6_chap4.php).
- **From above:** use sturdy verge boards and a legible smoke opening; let footprint and carpentry distinguish the hall.

## DECISIONS

- **Kawara face:** plain/cloud relief; authentic and suitable as a reusable default, while oni faces remain valid alternatives.
- **Chigi and katsuogi:** no on this unspecified irimoya hall; add only for a documented shrine-building prototype.
- **Buddhist tile colour:** green; yellow would imply appropriate imperial status or patronage.
- **Chiwen:** omit under the current no-figures rule; historically appropriate if architectural guardians become an exception.
- **Church cross:** yes, without a corpus; accurate religious identity without figural imagery.
- **Rus’ church roof:** modest onion for a late-sixteenth-century variant; neither helmet nor tent is a universal replacement across the full period.
- **Norse dragon finials:** omit by default; the claim that such imagery belongs exclusively to later stave churches is too strong.
