**One additional readability fix:** make the existing slopes continuous architectural playing surfaces, with the two rings visibly **vertical and attached to opposing side walls**, rather than freestanding on seating. That orientation is historically supported (**95%**, Durán; Wood); stronger ballcourt recognition is my design inference (**85%**).

**Things to AVOID:**

- Treating Borgia’s ritual imagery—or early-colonial Mendoza/Magliabechiano—as measured plans of Tenochtitlan.
- Modern centre circles, numbered lanes or elaborate boundary grids presented as attested.
- Calendar stones, deity faces or directional cosmograms used as interchangeable decoration.
- Classic Veracruz ceremonial stone equipment presented as everyday Mexica playing gear.
- Basketball-style horizontal hoops, stitched/inflatable balls, or conspicuously lumpy rubber.
- Gore, skull racks, sacrifice imagery, or claims that every match ended in sacrifice.
