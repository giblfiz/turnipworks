### Comparison of Top and Bottom Rows

No, the top-row icons do not look like they belong to the same set as the bottom-row icons. While they share a similar painterly art style, their function and construction are fundamentally different.

Here are the key differences:

*   **Kind of Picture (Composition):**
    *   **Top Row:** These are icons of individual **props or objects**. They are shown floating in space without any environmental context, like items you might find in an inventory or a build menu.
    *   **Bottom Row:** These are isometric **dioramas or room sections**. They depict fully realized corners of rooms, complete with walls, floors, and multiple objects arranged within that architectural space.

*   **Weight (Detail and Complexity):**
    *   **Top Row:** The icons are simpler and have less visual weight. They are single, focused assets.
    *   **Bottom Row:** The icons are far more complex and visually "heavy." They are composed of many different assets (walls, furniture, decorations, lighting) combined into a single, detailed scene.

*   **Finish (Lighting and Perspective):**
    *   **Top Row:** The lighting is simple and uniform, designed to make the object clear. They mostly have a simple drop shadow to separate them from the background.
    *   **Bottom Row:** The lighting is integrated into the scene itself. Torches and braziers cast light and shadow on the surrounding walls and objects, creating a much more finished and atmospheric look. The perspective is a strict isometric grid, which is essential for a room-builder.

In summary, the **top row shows the "what"** (individual items to place), while the **bottom row shows the "where"** (pre-designed or example rooms built from those items). They are thematically related but serve different purposes in a user interface.
