**Japan, Kyoto c.1850:** A coherent roster with useful civilian variety. Full armour is period-appropriate but represents military readiness or ceremonial display, not ordinary streetwear. The onmyōji is a sound choice: Edo-period practice included divination and almanac production; replace the unsupported blank divination board with a closed almanac. The shinobi needs a more honest fiction label and less modern ninja weaponry. Both women’s occupations are plausible; a woman trained in household defence would also be possible, but a routinely serving, fully armoured woman samurai needs an explicit fantasy qualification. [Period armour](https://searchcollection.asianart.org/objects/988/gusokutype-armor), [National Museum of Japanese History’s onmyōji exhibition](https://archive.rekihaku.ac.jp/english/exhibitions/project/old/231003/index.html).

**China, Beijing c.1770:** The strongest correction is the official: a blank square is not valid rank insignia, and robe colour, beads and hat finial cannot be assembled independently of status. Informal official dress solves this within your constraints. Reduce the noblewoman’s later-Qing-looking hair and platform silhouette without claiming that every two-part hairstyle was absent in 1770. The swordswoman’s literary framing is honest. The Daoist caster is appropriate when identified as a ritual specialist using ritual implements, rather than a fighting wizard. Women vendors or textile traders could expand ordinary female representation. [Qing rank badge](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1948.71), [Smithsonian explanation of court dress and status](https://asia.si.edu/education/educator-resources/teaching-china-with-the-smithsonian/interactives/images/summer-chaofu-formal-court-dress-for-a-top-rank-prince/).

An inactive puppet held in the hands solves both historical and technical problems. The women are plausible; their precise jewellery and water-pot arrangement should be treated as reconstruction, not a documented uniform. An armed Rajput noblewoman can be an exceptional fantasy character, but examples from other regions or centuries would not establish routine service here. The farm manager is plausible; a woman trader would also be a reasonable addition.
