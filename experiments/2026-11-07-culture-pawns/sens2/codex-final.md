**IND — Jaipur–Amber:** Keep the race/role assignments, including the tiefling astrologer. “Rajasthani” is much better than “Hindu” for this geographical dress pack; neither appearance nor profession establishes a character’s religion. The studded coat has a credible historical basis, and the astrolabe is particularly appropriate to Jaipur. I would use a paper manuscript bundle for the court scholar and ordinary court footwear rather than giving every learned person wooden sandals. These are refinements, not reasons to redesign the pack. [Armour example](https://staging-almadar.biennale.org.sa/en/objects/25-G5-85-0811), [Jaipur astrolabe](https://www.mhs.ox.ac.uk/astrolabe/catalogue/browseReport/Astrolabe_ID%3D184.html).

**JPN — Kyoto:** Keep the onmyōji, including the tiefling and dragonborn: the profession belongs in Edo Japan, not just the Heian period. Describe the elaborate outfit as a formal ceremonial interpretation, rather than every diviner’s everyday clothing. The physician needs a separate outfit; changing only the hat leaves them dressed as the diviner. Distinguish the two samurai kit identifiers, and replace the spy’s wakizashi with a tantō if the production requirement really is a *short* blade. Armoured women are a legitimate fantasy adaptation; do not market their distribution as a reconstruction of historical military demographics. [National Museum of Japanese History exhibition](https://archive.rekihaku.ac.jp/english/exhibitions/project/old/231003/index.html).

**CHN — Beijing:** This pack needs the most correction. Separate the physician’s and geomancer’s clothes from the Daoist specialist’s; overlapping fields do not make these occupations interchangeable. Keep the tiefling priest as a sincere practitioner, without an “evil priest” joke. The blank robe is a simplified representation, not a reproduction of full liturgical vestments, whose imagery carries meaning. Replace the butterfly knives as a conservative choice for Beijing in 1770, and resolve the upright jian rogue’s conflict with your low-blade requirement. [Daoist vestment context](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/69856), [research on butterfly swords’ nineteenth-century evidence](https://chinesemartialstudies.com/2017/02/13/an-updated-and-revised-social-history-of-the-hudiedao-butterfly-swords/).

**NRS — Scandinavia:** No race-specific exclusion is needed, including for the male tiefling seer. The staff has archaeological analogues, although their interpretation and original lengths are not uniformly certain. The shared caster gown and bead strand make a particular clothing choice look like a universal occupational uniform; offer a tunic-and-trousers alternative without policing which characters may wear which. Keep the lyre as a fantasy poet-musician’s attribute, not a claim that every historical skald played one. Tiefling horns must visibly emerge from the body rather than become “Viking helmet horns.” [Staff evidence](https://historiska.se/en/explore-history/history-hub/the-klinta-staff-a-viking-age-magic-wand/), [clothing evidence](https://en.natmus.dk/historical-knowledge/denmark/prehistoric-period-until-1050-ad/the-viking-age/the-people/clothes-and-jewellery/).

**MDE — Mamluk Cairo–Damascus:** The scholarly and medical roles are a strong choice; retain the orc physician and dragonborn astrologer. Lamellar over mail is defensible for the period, and straight swords should stay. “Mamluk” works as the pack’s dynastic label, but a civilian physician or thief is not thereby a member of the mamluk military establishment. Clarify that distinction in the pack description. Nothing about the turban, astrology or alchemy requires a tiefling-specific substitution or a change to its skin colour. [Research on medieval regional armour finds](https://www.ifao.egnet.net/uploads/publications/enligne/RAPH037.pdf).

**RUS — Novgorod–Kyiv:** Keep the restrained dress, retinue equipment and practical healer roles; I see no necessary race-specific change. Preserve “Rus’,” rather than relabelling the whole pack “Russian,” and name both anchor cities in the catalogue. The absence of crosses is acceptable under your symbol constraint, but do not describe the setting as uniformly pagan: Christianity was an established part of its institutions and culture. A horned healer should not automatically become a folkloric devil or an anti-Christian figure. [Historical overview](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-russia/kievan-rus-10151125/D84DCD4BF4D67941D79924E171F84775).

**MES — Tenochtitlan:** Keep the orc jaguar warrior, tiefling day-count reader and dragonborn healer. Removing prestigious or learned roles from those races would undermine the premise. The main dress correction is the universal male-derived civilian template: add plain huipilli-and-cueitl outfits for women rather than defaulting to a loincloth and shoulder mantle. Preserve the jaguar suit as crafted military dress, not an animal pelt or evidence that the orc is feral. Keep divination and book-making recognisably skilled practices; do not add blood or sacrificial imagery to make the caster “read.” [Research on jaguar textiles](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/242/), [Nahua women’s clothing evidence](https://aztecglyphs.wired-humanities.org/content/cihuapipiltin-tk210v).

**Coded-race question:** The full grid substantially reduces the implication that a real people “are orcs,” but it does not automatically neutralise an isolated image, caption or game mechanic. These combinations are acceptable in principle when every ancestry receives equal dignity, craftsmanship, intelligence and moral agency. “Devil-blooded” does not have to mean evil, and fantasy ancestry does not invalidate sincere religious practice. Do not selectively remove sacred roles, recolour tieflings, or make colonised peoples’ kits less distinctive. Instead, keep anatomy consistent across packs, prohibit race-linked moral or intellectual penalties, and review actual thumbnails as well as the grid. “Rajasthani tiefling spear warrior,” “Mexica orc jaguar warrior,” and “Chinese tiefling Daoist priest” are usable names. This is a conditional design assessment, not a claim that every community member will respond identically.
