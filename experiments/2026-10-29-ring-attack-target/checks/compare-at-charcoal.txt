### Pair Analysis

#### 1. Attack (`A` vs. `B`)
* **Do both fit?** **Yes, both fit** the `R1`–`R4` set well—both match the disc size, dark slate-charcoal ground, cool grey beveled rim, top-left lighting direction, and painterly style.
* **Set consistency (`R1`–`R4`):** **B belongs better.**
  * **Proportions & brushwork:** `R1`–`R4` use stout, chunky, slightly simplified tabletop proportions (`R4`'s thick padlock, `R2`'s broad heart, `R1`'s compact pawn). **B**'s wider, stockier blades and sturdier crossguards/hilts match that shape language, whereas **A**'s blades are thinner and more elongated.
  * **Palette & lighting:** **B** uses a warm cream/ivory-steel highlight on the upper-left blade planes and softer mid-grey shadows that harmonize with the metal and warm highlights in `R1`, `R3`, and `R4`. **A** uses starker, cooler icy-white highlights and harsher contrast.
  * **Ground & rim:** **B** has a cleaner double-bevel rim matching `R1`/`R4` and leaves a balanced dark-ground margin around the blade tips and pommels, while **A**'s longer blades crowd closer to the rim.
* **Readability at 31 px:** **B reads better.** Its broader blades and chunkier hilt shapes retain more bright surface area and form a heavier, clearer X-silhouette at 31 px, whereas **A**'s narrower blades thin out.
* **Winner:** **B**

#### 2. Target (`C` vs. `D`)
