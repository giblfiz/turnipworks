### Evaluation

**1. Same artist and set as R1–R4 (ground, rim, painting style, palette, disc size)?**
Yes across all four. All candidates share the exact 62 px / 31 px disc footprint, warm hand-painted bevels, dark recess outlines, and muted palette (the amber grips echo R3's arrow and R4's brass; the steel and charcoal match R4). Because A–D use essentially the same painted crossed-swords motif, they differ mainly in subtle rim and ground rendering:
- **`A`**: Matches well; slightly thicker, more textured rim (closer to `R2`) and a faintly mottled charcoal ground.
- **`B`**: Matches well; slightly thinner, dimmer grey rim with a mottled dark ground.
- **`C`**: Best set match; clean, bright cool-slate rim (matching `R1`/`R3`/`R4`) paired with a deep charcoal ground for maximum contrast.
- **`D`**: Strong set match; clean cool-slate rim like `R1`/`R3`/`R4` with a slightly flatter, lighter slate interior (closer to `R3`).

**2. Does it read as the intended action at 31 px?**
- **Yes, for all four (`A`–`D`).** The bright silver-white blades form a high-contrast `X` against the dark ground, and the warm amber hilts clearly anchor the orientation at 31 px.

**3. Single best candidate & regeneration check:**
- **Best candidate: `C`** (crispest cool-slate rim matching `R1`/`R3`/`R4` and highest blade-to-ground contrast at 31 px).
- **Needs regeneration? No.** It is production-ready as-is. The only minor caveats—neither requiring regeneration unless they bother you—are that the pommels slightly overlap the bottom rim edge, and crossed swords are a symmetric "combat/duel" symbol rather than a directional single-sword slash ("this pawn attacks").
