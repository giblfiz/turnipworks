### Step 1: Labels and Orientations (Image by Image)

For every image, each visible label is listed below alongside a description of the prop above it and its orientation classification, using exactly one of the required categories:
* `UPRIGHT (standing as it would in a room; we see its top)`
* `ON ITS SIDE (tipped over 90 degrees; we see its side profile)`
* `ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP (its front or face looks up at the camera)`
* `FACE DOWN (its back, rear or underside is toward the camera)`
* `UPSIDE DOWN`
* `UNCLEAR`

### Step 2: Catalog Usability Evaluation

#### 1. Objects that are NOT laid out sensibly for a catalog:

* **Seating tipped onto its side:**
  * **`Stool bar`** (`view-1`, `view-2`, `view-3`): Tipped 90° onto its side with its tall legs sprawling horizontally across the floor plane. A bar stool should be **`UPRIGHT`**, showing its circular cushion from above just like the small round tables and armchairs.
  * **`Chair armed · high seat`** (`view-6`, `view-7`): Lying on its side showing an elevation profile, while every other armchair in the row stands upright showing its cushion. It should be **`UPRIGHT`**.

* **Pillars and columns knocked over:**
  * **`Pillar round`** (`view-4`, `view-5`, `view-6`, `view-7`) & **`Pillar square · black`** (`view-8`, `view-9`): Both are tipped 90° onto their side, lying flat across the plane like fallen debris. They represent standing architectural elements and should be placed **`UPRIGHT`** (or displayed in an angled 3D catalog preview tile so their height can be seen without sprawling across neighboring grid slots).
  * *Special failure case —* **`Pillar square`** (`view-8`, `view-9`): Although technically `UPRIGHT`, viewed from straight down it appears as a featureless, flat gray square block. A user cannot tell if it is an 8-foot pillar or a floor stepping tile. It should either be tilted slightly in a preview portrait or lie on its back/side with a consistent catalog convention.

* **Floor / Tabletop lights knocked onto their sides:**
  * **`Candelabra · fine`** (`view-7`), **`Candelabra · common`** (`view-7`), **`Candlestand pricket · brass`** (`view-9`), **`Candlestick floor · iron`** (`view-9`), and **`Brazier standing`** (`view-7`): All of these are freestanding props tipped 90° over onto their side/tripod bases. They look knocked down or discarded. They should be standing **`UPRIGHT`** on their bases.
  * **`Candle tree · wood`** (`view-7`, `view-9`): The entire vertical support pole is laid flat along the ground plane. It should stand **`UPRIGHT`** on its base.

* **Wall lanterns and sconces shown in awkward side elevation:**
  * **`Lantern wall iron · common`** (`view-3`), **`Lantern pierced tin · cone`** (`view-3`), and **`Sconce reflector · tin`** (`view-3`): These are shown tipped sideways in profile, making them hard to recognize and inconsistent with the other wall sconces. They should be **`ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP`** (with their mounting plate flat on the ground plane so the face of the lantern/sconce looks directly up at the camera).

#### 2. Objects that look CLEARLY RIGHT:

* **Standard upright room furniture:**
  * All dining, feasting, and pub tables (`Table square`, `Table trestle`, `Table round small · common`, `Table round small`, `Table round small · fine`, `Table feast · fine`, `Table feast`): Their top surfaces are clean, recognizable, and immediately tell the user the table's size, shape, and finish.
  * All upright seating and benches (`Bench long`, `Bench stone`, `Pew`, `Settle`, `Throne`, `Faldstool`, `Chair plain · common`, `Chair plain · fine`, `Chair armed · fine`, `Chair armed`, `Chair armed · common`): Their seats and arms read clearly from above, matching how they will look when placed in a scene.
  * Bar counters (`Bar counter · straight common`, `Bar counter · corner common`, `Bar counter · end common`, `Bar counter · flap common`): Their top surfaces and bartender recesses are completely intuitive.
  * Freestanding floor props (`Dais`, `Crate`, `Barrel`, `Loot sack`, `Pulpit`, `Font holy`, `Altar stone · marble`, `Altar stone`, `Brazier large`, `Cheese wheel cut`, `Candlestick · common`): All sit upright on their bases and read unambiguously from above.
* **Tombs and crypt effigies:**
  * `Stone coffin lidless`, `Sarcophagus`, `Effigy knight`, `Coffin`, and `Monument urn draped`: Because coffins, sarcophagi, and knight effigies naturally rest horizontally on crypt floors, looking straight down at their sculpted lids, open skeletons, or carved armor is 100% natural and instantly identifiable.
* **Wall-hung props and tall facades intentionally laid `ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP`:**
  * `Fireplace wall · common`, `Fireplace wall · fine`, `Fireplace wall`, `Fireplace grand`: Showing the front hearth, mantel, and embers facing up allows users to distinguish between the styles; if they stood upright, the camera would only see an uninformative chimney mantel ledge.
