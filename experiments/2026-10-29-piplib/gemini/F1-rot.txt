# Step 2: Usability Judgment

### **Objects Laid in a Way That is NOT Sensible for a Catalog**

1. **`Sconce reflector · brass`** (`view-2.png`)
   - **What is wrong**: It is tipped **ON ITS SIDE** (rotated 90 degrees), displaying only its thin edge profile, side bracket, and candle pointing horizontally. While all sibling wall sconces (`Sconce reflector · tin`, `Sconce candle double · wood`, `Sconce candle double · iron`) are laid face-up on their back to reveal their full decorative reflector plates and candle positions, this one is turned sideways, making it distorted and hard to recognize.
   - **How it should lie**: **ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP**, with the circular brass reflector dish facing directly up at the camera (identical to `Sconce reflector · tin`).

2. **`Lantern hanging`** (`view-4.png`)
   - **What is wrong**: It stands **UPRIGHT**. Because the camera looks directly down from above, the viewer only sees the square sheet-metal pyramid roof and top eyelet. Its glass panes, candle flame, and frame are completely obscured under the roof, making it look like a featureless pyramid cap rather than an illuminated lantern. Every wall lantern is displayed face-up for clear recognition, but this hanging lantern is not.
   - **How it should lie**: **ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP** (tilted 90 degrees), so that its side elevation—showing the glass panes, candlelight, and metal cage—faces the camera.

3. **`Scales balance`** (`view-4.png`)
   - **What is wrong**: It is laid **ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP**, splayed completely flat on the floor as if it were a wall-hung sign or a knocked-over prop. A balance scale is a freestanding tabletop dressing item.
   - **How it should lie**: It should stand **UPRIGHT** on its base. In a top-down view, an upright balance scale clearly shows its central post, horizontal balance beam, and both round suspended pans in their true functional orientation for placing on shop counters or tables.

4. **`Pillar square`** and **`Pillar square · black`** (`view-3.png`)
   - **What is wrong**: They stand **UPRIGHT**. Seen straight down from above, without height cues, they collapse into featureless 2D flat squares (in the case of `Pillar square · black`, it looks like a missing texture or a solid black UI box). Unlike `Pillar round` which reveals both a round shaft and square base, these provide zero visual depth.
   - **How they should lie**: In an object-picker catalog, they should either be laid **ON THEIR SIDE** (or displayed with an isometric tilt/profile) so the column shaft, capital moldings, and base plinths are identifiable.

### **Objects That Look Clearly Right**

- **All Freestanding Tables, Chairs, Stools, and Benches**:
  (`Table square`, `Table round small · common`, `Table round small`, `Table round small · fine`, `Table trestle`, `Table feast`, `Table feast · fine`, `Chair plain · common`, `Chair plain · fine`, `Chair armed`, `Chair armed · fine`, `Chair armed · common`, `Chair armed · high seat`, `Stool bar`, `Settle`, `Faldstool`, `Bench long`, `Bench stone`, `Throne`).
