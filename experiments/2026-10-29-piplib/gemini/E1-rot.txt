### Step 1: Labels, Object Descriptions, and Orientations

Each image is taken from a top-down camera looking straight down at the ground plane. Orientations are categorized strictly using the specified definitions:
- **UPRIGHT**: Standing as it would in a room; we see its top surface.
- **ON ITS SIDE**: Tipped over 90 degrees; we see its side profile.
- **ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP**: Its front or face looks straight up at the camera.
- **FACE DOWN**: Its back, rear, or underside is toward the camera.
- **UPSIDE DOWN**: Inverted.
- **UNCLEAR**: Indeterminate.

### Step 2: Judgment of Catalog Layout

#### 1. Objects Laid in a Way That is NOT Sensible

* **`Notice board`** ([view-1.png](file:///tmp/piplib-gem-ADqS/view-1.png), [view-2.png](file:///tmp/piplib-gem-ADqS/view-2.png))
  * **What is wrong**: It is standing UPRIGHT. Because the camera is looking straight down, the board is seen edge-on. You only see a thin horizontal line and the tops of two post feet. The face of the board, its wooden frame, and the posted notices are completely hidden, making the asset unreadable and hard to recognize.
  * **How it should lie**: It should lie **ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP** (face up toward the camera), exactly like the wall sconces and wall shelves, so the catalog user can see the board face, paper notices, and frame.
* **`Pillar square · black`** ([view-8.png](file:///tmp/piplib-gem-ADqS/view-8.png), [view-9.png](file:///tmp/piplib-gem-ADqS/view-9.png))
  * **What is wrong**: It is lying **ON ITS SIDE** (tipped 90 degrees onto the plane), looking like a fallen ruin or log. In the exact same category, `Pillar square` and `Pillar round` stand UPRIGHT showing their tops. This is completely inconsistent and misleading for an intact architectural column.
  * **How it should lie**: It should stand **UPRIGHT** (standing as it would in a room, showing its top square plinth/capital), matching `Pillar square` and `Pillar round`.
* **`Lantern pierced tin · cone`** ([view-3.png](file:///tmp/piplib-gem-ADqS/view-3.png))
  * **What is wrong**: It is lying **ON ITS SIDE** (tipped over 90 degrees), displaying its side silhouette like a knocked-over prop. All other freestanding lighting fixtures (`Candlestick · common`, candelabras, braziers) stand upright.
  * **How it should lie**: If it is a tabletop/standing lantern, it should stand **UPRIGHT** (showing its top cone and ring handle). If it is intended as a wall fixture, it should lie **ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP** with its mounting hardware flat against the plane.
* **`Armor stand`** ([view-6.png](file:///tmp/piplib-gem-ADqS/view-6.png))
  * **What is wrong**: Standing UPRIGHT. Seen straight from above, all you see is the round top of a helmet and shoulder pauldrons, reading as an ambiguous, dark clump of metal/leather that is difficult to identify.
  * **How it should lie**: For catalog identification, it would be much clearer lying **ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP** (similar to `Rite mummified remains` and `Effigy knight`), revealing the full suit of plate armor, breastplate, and weapons.

#### 2. Objects That Look Clearly Right

* **Freestanding floor furniture, tables, and seating (UPRIGHT)**:
  * All tables (`Table square`, `Table trestle`, `Table round small · common/fine`, `Table feast / fine`)
  * All seating (`Stool bar`, `Chair plain · common/fine`, `Chair armed · common/fine/high seat`, `Settle`, `Bench long`, `Bench stone`, `Throne`, `Faldstool`, `Pew`, `Rite choir stall`)
  * Bar counters (`Bar counter · straight common`, `Bar counter · corner common`, `Bar counter · end common`, `Bar counter · flap common`)
  * Religious/sanctuary platforms (`Altar stone`, `Altar stone · marble`, `Dais`, `Pulpit`, `Lectern`, `Font holy`, `Prayer candle rack`, `Rite alms box`)
  * Funerary props (`Stone coffin lidless`, `Sarcophagus`, `Effigy knight`, `Coffin`, `Monument urn draped`)
  * Storage and tabletop objects (`Crate`, `Loot sack`, `Barrel`, `Cheese wheel cut`)
  * Standing lights (`Candlestick · common`, `Candlestand pricket · brass`, `Candlestick floor · iron`, `Candelabra · fine/common`, `Candle tree · brass/wood/iron`, `Brazier large`, `Brazier standing`, `Chandelier ring`, `Chandelier ring · great`)
  * Architectural pillars (`Pillar round`, `Pillar square`)  
  *Why they are right*: Seen from above, their top silhouettes are cleanly recognizable and match exactly how players and DMs view and manipulate tokens on a top-down battlemap.
* **Wall-mounted fixtures and vertical facades (ON THEIR BACK, FRONT UP)**:
  * Wall lights and sconces (`Sconce reflector · brass/tin`, `Sconce candle single/double/triple · brass/iron`, `Torch bracket fine/rough · brass/iron`, `Torch sconce`)
  * Wall fixtures and architectural facades (`Shelf wall`, `Fireplace wall · common/fine/grand`, `Back bar shelves`, `Bard instrument stand`, `Cask rack`, `Confessional`, `Organ pipe`)  
  *Why they are right*: These items have their primary functional details on their vertical front face. If stood upright against an invisible wall, a top-down camera would only see a thin sliver of a mantel or mounting bracket. Showing them face up makes them instantly recognizable in the catalog.
* **Recumbent remains (`Rite mummified remains`, ON THEIR BACK, FRONT UP)**:
  * A mummy/corpse naturally lies recumbent; lying face up makes the bandages and form immediately recognizable, whereas standing upright would only show the top of a head.
