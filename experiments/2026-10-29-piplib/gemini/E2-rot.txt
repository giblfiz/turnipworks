### Step 2: Judgment for a Top-Down Prop Catalog

In a top-down virtual tabletop catalog viewed from straight above, props need to be legible based on where their defining visual features live. 

#### **1. Objects Laid in a Way That is NOT Sensible**

* **`Alch reagent shelf tall` (`view-2.png`)**
  * **What is wrong:** It is placed `UPRIGHT` (standing vertically as it would against a room wall). Because the camera looks straight down from the ceiling, all that is visible is the narrow, flat wooden roof board (cornice) and a few unidentifiable specks along the edge. The entire body of the shelf—its vertical compartments, shelves, potion vials, and reagent jars—is hidden underneath. It looks like an anonymous wooden 2×4 plank rather than a reagent bookcase.
  * **How it should lie instead:** It should lie **`ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP`** (tilted 90° so its front face looks directly up at the camera). This matches how `Apothecary drawers · shop`, `Apothecary drawers`, and all the `Shop shelf stocked ...` props are laid out, allowing the user to immediately see the shelves and contents.

* **`Alch specimen jar large` (`view-1.png`, `view-2.png`)**
  * **What is wrong:** Standing `UPRIGHT`, the camera looks directly down through the cork stopper and tied burlap wrapping. The primary identifying feature of a specimen jar—the transparent glass body and the preserved creature suspended inside—is obscured by the opaque top lid. It reads as an ambiguous circular burlap bundle or nest.
  * **How it should lie instead:** While smaller jars can stand upright if open, a tall enclosed specimen jar is far more recognizable tilted or laid **`ON ITS SIDE`** (or angled), so the glass cylinder and specimen inside are visible in profile.

#### **2. Objects That Look Clearly Right**

* **Floor furniture and working surfaces (`UPRIGHT`):**
  * All shop counters (`Counter shop · bakery / tailor / general / apoth / curio / smith`), prep tables (`Table kitchen prep`, `Table kitchen prep · great`), workbenches (`Alch bench dressed`), and craft display tables (`Craft display table goods · ...`). From straight down, their primary identifying surfaces (tabletops loaded with tools, bread, ingots, herbs) are 100% visible and intuitive.
  * Seating (`Stool three leg`). Seen from above, the circular seat and three splayed leg feet provide an unmistakable top-down silhouette.
* **Open containers, vehicles, and floor piles (`UPRIGHT`):**
  * `Alch cauldron bubbling green`, `Alch potion crate straw`, `Baskets stack`, `Shop floor stock · ...`, `Spice sacks open`, `Mil catapult stones`, `Rural ore bucket`, and `Bucket`. Looking directly down into their open tops shows the boiling liquid, potion bottles, spices, water, and cargo.
  * `Wheelbarrow` and `Mine cart`. Looking down into the open hopper/tray immediately identifies them.
  * `Collapse rubble`, `Rubble pile`, `Rural tailings pile`, `Rural pit props stack`, and `Rope coil`. These are native floor scatter; top-down is their natural orientation.
  * `Alch still copper large` and `Alch athanor`. Their distinctive copper domes, swan necks, and flue towers read cleanly from above.
* **Wall-hung props and vertical displays deliberately laid `ON THEIR BACK, FRONT UP`:**
  * `Apothecary drawers · shop` and `Apothecary drawers`: Laying them on their backs exposes the entire multi-drawer front facade, making them instantly recognizable.
  * All wall/shop display shelves (`Shop shelf stocked · curio / apoth / tailor / bakery / smith / general` and `Shelving`): Laying them on their backs allows the user to see every shelf tier and thematic item.
  * `Alch formula chalkboard` and `Mine tool rack`: Laying them on their backs exposes the written formulas, chalk tray, and hanging mining picks/shovels.
  * Wall lights (`Lantern wall brass · common`, `Lantern wall iron · fine`, etc.): Laying them on their backplates allows the full lantern housing, bracket, and glass panes to be evaluated before picking.
