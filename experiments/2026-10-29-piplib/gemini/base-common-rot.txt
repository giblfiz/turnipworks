Here is every readable label from each view image, the object above it, and its orientation as seen from the straight-down camera, using exclusively the required taxonomy:
- **UPRIGHT** (standing as it would in a room; we see its top)
- **ON ITS SIDE** (tipped over 90 degrees; we see its side profile)
- **ON ITS BACK, FRONT UP** (its front or face looks up at the camera)
- **FACE DOWN** (its back, rear or underside is toward the camera)
- **UPSIDE DOWN**
- **UNCLEAR**

### Step 2: Judgment and Recommendations

#### 1. Objects Laid in a Way That Is NOT Sensible for a Prop Catalog

These objects suffer from coordinate mismatches (often typical 3D mesh import orientation bugs like Y-up vs. Z-up) or awkward placements that make them look knocked over, hide their function, or would spawn them on the floor when dragged into a top-down scene.

##### A. Staircases Tipped Sideways (Critical Flaw)
* **`Stair half`**, **`Stair straight`**, **`Stair grand`**, **`Stair exterior wood`**, **`Stair spiral wide`**, **`Stair landing`**
  * **What is wrong:** Every single one of these staircases is knocked over 90 degrees onto its side stringer or structural framing. In a top-down tabletop view, you cannot see the steps, their rise/run, or their grid footprint. `Stair spiral wide` looks like a collapsed pile of lumber, and `Stair landing` looks like an abstract wooden bracket. Furthermore, dragging them from the catalog into a room would place a fallen staircase lying on the floor.
  * **How they should lie:** They should stand **UPRIGHT** on their base, exactly as **`Stair wall hugging`** does, so the user clearly sees the steps, landings, and floor-plan footprint from above.

##### B. Furniture & Stands Tipped Sideways
* **`Trade ale taster stool`**
  * **What is wrong:** The stool is knocked onto its side with its legs sticking out horizontally. It looks like tavern debris after a brawl rather than a piece of seating ready to be placed at a table.
  * **How it should lie:** It should stand **UPRIGHT** on its legs, showing the round seat cushion from above.
* **`Craft saddle horse stand`**
  * **What is wrong:** Tipped 90 degrees onto its side frame, hiding its saddle-holding beam.
  * **How it should lie:** It should stand **UPRIGHT** on its four splayed legs with its top rail running horizontally.

##### C. Architectural Rooftop Elements & Posts Tipped Sideways
* **`Roof cistern`**
  * **What is wrong:** A water cistern on a heavy wooden trestle stand tipped onto its side, with the timber legs sticking out sideways into empty space.
  * **How it should lie:** It should stand **UPRIGHT** on its timber legs.
* **`Chimney stack`** & **`Roof bartizan`**
  * **What is wrong:** A brick chimney and a stone turret are lying horizontally flat on the ground.
  * **How they should lie:** They should stand **UPRIGHT** (chimney showing its top flues/openings; turret pointing up).
* **`Fence post corner`**
  * **What is wrong:** A vertical wooden corner post lying horizontally like a fallen log.
  * **How it should lie:** It should stand **UPRIGHT** as a square vertical post.

##### D. Storage Containers Rolling on Their Sides
* **`Barrel`**, **`Keg small`**
  * **What is wrong:** Both are tipped 90 degrees, rolling on their curved bellies (with `Keg small` having its spigot pinned at the bottom). While horizontal barrels on dedicated racks exist, standalone barrels and kegs in tabletop catalogs should sit on their flat round bases unless designated as "spilled/broken."
  * **How they should lie:** They should stand **UPRIGHT** on their round ends, matching **`Crate`**, **`Trade bread crate delivery`**, and **`Mash tun`**.

##### E. Inconsistent and Tipped Tableware, Drinkware & Table Lighting
* **`Tankard wooden`**, **`Tankard horn`**, **`Cup leather`**, **`Stein stoneware`**, **`Mug of ale`**, **`Pitcher pewter`**, **`Bottle wicker`**, **`Bottle wine green`**, **`Jug of cider`**, **`Kettle`**, **`Brew copper`**, **`Candle`**, **`Candlestick`**
  * **What is wrong:** There is a glaring internal contradiction in the catalog: **`Mug large`**, **`Bowl wood large`**, **`Bowl pewter`**, **`Plate ceramic`**, and **`Dutch oven`** stand upright, while almost all other mugs, tankards, flagons, kettles, and candles are tipped 90 degrees onto their sides! 
    - A tipped `Candle` or `Candlestick` looks like an active fire hazard fallen on a table.
    - Tipped mugs and flagons look like spilled, discarded drinkware. When dragged onto a tabletop map, they will spawn knocked over on the table.
  * **How they should lie:** All standing tableware, kettles, and table candle holders should stand **UPRIGHT** on their bases (so we see down into the mugs/kettles from above), just like `Mug large` and `Dutch oven`. *(Bottles could optionally remain horizontal only if intended as rolling bottles, but upright is standard for placed props).*

##### F. Wall-Hung Lights Not Shown Face Up
* **`Lantern wall iron`**, **`Sconce reflector`**
  * **What is wrong:** Both are wall-hung fixtures, but unlike **`Sconce candle double`** and **`Candle box sconce`** (which lie face up), these two are shown from their side profiles. You see the side bracket rather than the front decorative face, glass, or reflector dish.
  * **How they should lie:** They should lie **ON THEIR BACK, FRONT UP**, facing the camera directly, matching `Sconce candle double`.

##### G. Standing Flagpole and Weathervane
* **`Roof flagpole`**, **`Weathervane`**
  * **What is wrong:** Tipped 90 degrees onto the plane. While lying on the ground allows the user to see the flag/arrow silhouette, dropping these props into a 3D scene places a fallen flagpole or toppled weathervane.
  * **How they should lie:** They should stand **UPRIGHT** in their true 3D orientation.

#### 2. Objects That Look CLEARLY RIGHT

These objects are oriented sensibly, read immediately from a straight-down catalog view, and match tabletop expectations:

1. **Top-Down Tableware, Cookware & Open Containers:**
   * **`Bowl wood large`**, **`Bowl pewter`**, **`Plate ceramic`**, **`Platter`**, **`Platter board handled`**, **`Dutch oven`**, **`Mash tun`**, **`Trade fermenting crock`**, **`Trade yeast crock`**, **`Rural saddle soap tin`**, **`Fish gutting board`**, **`Mug large`**, **`Trade bread crate delivery`**, **`Crate`**, **`Loot sack`**
   * *Why:* Sitting upright on their bases; the user looks directly down into them, their contents and rims are instantly recognizable, and they drop cleanly onto tables or floors.

2. **Prepared Food & Meals:**
   * **`Beans bowl`**, **`Soup bowl`**, **`Rice bowl`**, **`Rabbit roast`**, **`Bread round loaf cut`**, **`Bread burnt`**, **`Pie`**, **`long loaf torn`**, **`ut smoked`**
   * *Why:* Resting naturally on their platters/bowls/boards; top-down views display their prepared food cleanly.

3. **Flat Floor & Tabletop Goods:**
   * **`Rug wolfskin`**, **`Rural horse blanket folded`**, **`Napkin folded`**, **`Knucklebones`**, **`Dice and cup`**, **`Wineskin full`**, **`Chandelier ring`**
   * *Why:* Designed to lie flat on the floor, table, or hang overhead as a horizontal ring; their top surface is their natural presentation.

4. **Weapons, Tools & Documents Shown Face Up:**
   * **`Sword long`**, **`Dagger`**, **`Proclamation scroll`**, **`Bard playbill`**, **`Bounty poster`**, **`Fiddle`**, **`Flask hip`**, **`Trade brewers paddle`**, **`Trade bung mallet`**, **`Trade oven rake`**, **`Feather duster`**
   * *Why:* Laid flat with front/blades/strings/text facing straight up at the camera. In a top-down view, this maximizes silhouette recognition and legibility.

5. **Properly Oriented Wall Fixtures (Shown Face Up):**
   * **`Candle box sconce`**, **`Sconce candle double`**, **`Shelf wall`**
   * *Why:* As wall fixtures, displaying them flat on their back with front facing up lets the user see the candles, metalwork, and shelf items clearly.

6. **True Top-Down Architectural Elements & Terrain:**
   * **`Stair wall hugging`**, **`Hedge straight`**, **`Hedge end block`**, **`Wall drystone gate`**, **`Roof hatch`**, and all terrain tiles (**`Cave rock`**, **`Drain grate`**, **`Cobblestone dungeon`**, **`Lawn mown`**, **`Terracotta`**, **`Water flooded`**)
   * *Why:* They represent the floor-plan footprint directly on the grid, ready for map layout.

*(Note on Doors, Windows, and Fences in `view-5.png`: Items like **`Door barn double`**, **`Door (reinforced)`**, **`Door cell bars`**, **`Door (plank)`**, **`Wall`**, **`Window`**, **`Portcullis`**, **`Fence picket`**, and **`Balustrade stone`** lie on their backs, front up. For catalog recognition, this is necessary because looking straight down at an upright door shows only a thin 2-inch sliver; however, the library system must ensure they auto-erect upright upon placement.)*
