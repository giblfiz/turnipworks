**1. LIST FIRST (view-1):**
* **Shapes:** Repeating undulating, ribbon-like horizontal/diagonal curves (swirls/sweeps); small, sharp, jagged irregular spots; thin branching cracks.
* **Colours:** Cream, off-white, and warm sandy beige base; high-contrast ochre/caramel-brown for the jagged spots; soft tan shadows.
* **Size and direction:** Sweeps run horizontally across each ~5-ft cell with a slight diagonal tilt; jagged brown patches (~1 ft wide) sit in the exact same relative spot, aligned in a rigid 6×4 array across the entire surface.

**2. BLIND NAME (view-1):**
* **Material & setting:** Reads as patterned linoleum, printed vinyl, or decorative ceramic tile from a 1970s retro/modern interior or stylized Middle Eastern bathhouse rather than exterior roofing.
* **Three words for a map key:** **Patterned Linoleum Floor** (or **Chipped Plaster Roof**).

**3. RENDERING (view-1):**
* It attempts hand-painted brushwork, but the fine cracks and tight strokes turn into busy, high-frequency visual noise at table distance (unlike the broad, calm, readable planes of view-2).
* **5-ft repeat:** Yes, glaringly so. The high-contrast brown spot acts as a tracking marker that reveals a stark 6×4 polka-dot grid across the roof.

**4. INTENT VERDICT (view-1):**
* **Verdict:** **BAD**
* **Single biggest reason:** Catastrophic tiling repetition ("wallpaper effect"). Baking a high-contrast, distinct visual anchor (the jagged brown worn patch) into a single 5-ft tile turns what should be an organic, monolithic desert plaster surface into a rigid, distracting checkerboard of 24 identical clones.
