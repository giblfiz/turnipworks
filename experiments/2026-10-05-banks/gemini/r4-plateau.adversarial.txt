The LAND in this scene looks overtly computer-generated. Its smoothness and the perfection of its forms are the primary giveaways. It lacks the chaotic, fractal complexity of natural terrain or even hand-sculpted wargaming terrain.

Here are the tells, in order of how much they damage the illusion of naturalism:

1.  **The Hill's Unnaturally Perfect Shape.** The central hill is the most significant flaw. It looks like it was created with a "soft selection" or "proportional editing" tool in a 3D modeling program. It's a perfectly smooth, symmetrical, Gaussian-like bump. Real hills have irregularities: small ridges, flatter spots, steeper sections, and an uneven footprint. This is a mathematical curve, not a landform.
    *   **Where:** The entire raised landform in the center.
    *   **Views:** Both. Its perfect roundness is obvious from the high table-distance view, and its unnaturally smooth profile is the first thing you notice in the low view.

2.  **The Grid's Flawless Deformation.** The grid lines wrap over the perfect curve of the hill with mathematical precision. This is something that is trivial to do in 3D software but nearly impossible to achieve by hand. A hand-made model would either have the grid painted on (showing brush stroke imperfections) or the terrain would be terraced/stepped to approximate the slope while keeping the grid squares flat. This fluid, perfect projection is a dead giveaway of a digital mesh.
    *   **Where:** All grid lines on the sloped surface of the hill.
    *   **Views:** Both, but it is most blatantly artificial in the low view where you can clearly see the lines bending in a perfect, continuous arc.

3.  **The "Floating" Scatter Objects.** The rocks, bushes, and trees sit *on top* of the grass texture. They don't look integrated. There is no disturbed earth, no pressed-down grass, and no subtle blending at their base. They look like digital assets that have been placed on a surface without affecting the surface itself, lacking any sense of weight or interaction with the ground they are supposed to be rooted in or resting on.
    *   **Where:** The base of every rock, bush, and tree.
    *   **Views:** Primarily the low view, where the lack of integration at the base of the objects is most apparent.

4.  **Uniform, Painted-On Textures.** The ground texture is very uniform. The grass is a single, repeating pattern, and the brown patches look like decals or a different texture painted on with a soft brush in a program. There is no change in the *physicality* of the terrain—no change in grass length, no actual soil, just a color change. This makes the ground feel like a flat image wrapped over a 3D shape.
    *   **Where:** The entire ground plane, especially the brown patches on the hill.
    *   **Views:** Both. The high view shows the repetitive nature of the texture, while the low view makes the "painted on" quality of the brown patches more obvious.
