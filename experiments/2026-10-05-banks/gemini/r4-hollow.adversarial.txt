Yes, the land looks overwhelmingly computer-generated, smooth, and artificial. It does not convincingly emulate hand-made miniature terrain or natural ground. The tells are numerous and significant.

Here are the giveaways, in order of how much they damage the illusion:

1.  **The Grid Deformation.** This is the single most damning element. The grid is not painted onto a surface; it's part of the texture that is mathematically wrapped around the 3D model of the terrain. This creates perfectly smooth, continuous lines that curve over the hills and into the hollows in a way that is physically impossible for a real-world grid. It screams "UV-mapped texture on a 3D mesh."
    *   **Where:** Most obviously in the central depression in `hollow-table.png` and along the crests of the hills in `hollow-low.png`.
    *   **Views:** Both, but it is most pronounced and artificial-looking in the low view where the curvature is exaggerated.

2.  **The "Soft Body" Terrain Contours.** The hills and depressions lack any geological or hand-sculpted logic. They are perfectly smooth, rounded, and lack the sharp edges, small-scale noise, erosion patterns, or tool marks characteristic of either natural terrain or physical miniatures. The landscape looks like a flat plane that was pushed and pulled by a large, soft digital brush in a 3D modeling program.
    *   **Where:** The main central depression and all the surrounding hills. Look at the perfectly smooth curve of the hill in the foreground of `hollow-low.png`.
    *   **Views:** Both, but the unnatural smoothness is especially apparent in the low view.

3.  **Decal-Like Ground Patches.** The brown dirt patches and denser foliage areas look like decals or a secondary texture painted on with zero blending. There is an unnaturally sharp, clean edge between the grass and the dirt. In reality, there would be a transition zone with thinning grass, loose soil, and pebbles spilling over. Here, it's just a color swap.
    *   **Where:** All over the map. For a clear example, see the dirt patch to the left of the central red pawn in `hollow-table.png`.
    *   **Views:** Both.

4.  **"Floating" Scatter Terrain.** The rocks, trees, and bushes appear to be placed *on top of* the ground, not *in* it. Their bases don't disturb the terrain; there is no displaced earth, no buildup of fallen leaves or soil, and the grass texture clips directly into their base. They look like separate digital assets dropped onto the surface from a library.
    *   **Where:** Every single rock and tree. Look at the large rock near the yellow pawn in `hollow-low.png`. It's just sitting on the grass grid with no interaction.
    *   **Views:** Both, but more noticeable in the low view.
