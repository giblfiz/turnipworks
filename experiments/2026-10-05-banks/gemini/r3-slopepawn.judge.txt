Here is an analysis of the two methods for marking a game piece's space on a slope.

### RIGHT (Rigid Disc Base)
*   **Does it look right or wrong?** It looks wrong from a physical or realistic standpoint, but correct from a traditional tabletop game perspective.
*   **Why?** The base is a rigid, level object placed on an unlevel surface. This is a common visual compromise in games, but it breaks the illusion that the character is standing *in* the world.
*   **Is anything buried or hanging?** Yes. The uphill side of the black disc is clearly clipping into and buried by the grassy hill. The downhill side is hanging in the air, with a visible gap between the base and the ground.

### LEFT (Painted Ring)
*   **Does it look right or wrong?** It looks physically right and well-integrated into the environment.
*   **Why?** The ring conforms to the contour of the ground, exactly as a circle painted on a hill would. The figure's feet are planted firmly on the surface.
*   **Is anything buried or hanging?** No. Both the figure and its marker are perfectly flush with the terrain.

### Comparison and Conclusion
*   **Believability:** The **left** piece is far more believable. It creates a sense of place and makes the character look like they are actually standing on the hill, rather than being a game token placed on top of a map. The rigid base on the right constantly reminds the player of the artifice of the game board.
*   **Clarity of Space:** The painted ring on the **left** still reads clearly as "this piece's space." While it appears as an ellipse from this camera angle due to perspective and the slope, its function as a circular area marker on the ground is immediately understandable. The consistent color and its position encircling the figure leave no room for ambiguity about its purpose.
