Yes, being honest and adversarial, the land in this scene looks very clearly computer-generated and artificial. The underlying terrain geometry is too smooth and mathematically perfect, which betrays its digital origin, even with the scatter of more natural-looking assets on top.

Here are the specific tells, in order of how much they hurt the illusion:

1.  **The Perfectly Deformed Grid.** This is the single biggest giveaway. A grid on a real, physical tabletop model would be painted on, and the lines would stretch and distort imperfectly over hills, or the grid squares themselves would be built into the terrain. Here, the grid is a texture map applied to a 3D model, causing the lines to curve with mathematical precision over every contour. It's a feature that can *only* exist in a digital environment.
    *   **Where:** Everywhere on the ground. It's most obvious on the slopes of the central hills and inside the depression on the left.
    *   **View:** Both. It's starkly clear in the high view and equally apparent in the low view, where the lines can be seen wrapping perfectly over the foreground hill.

2.  **Uniformly Smooth, 'Blobby' Hills.** The terrain lacks any fine or medium detail. The hills rise and fall with a perfectly smooth, consistent curvature, like a soft brush was used in a terrain editor or a simple noise function was generated. Real terrain has lumps, bumps, small ridges, erosion lines, and other high-frequency details. These hills are just smooth, rounded mounds.
    *   **Where:** The entire landscape, but especially the large central hill and the rolling terrain in the background. The low-angle view highlights the perfect, sine-wave-like profile of the hills against the horizon.
    *   **View:** Both, but it's more damaging in the low view where the smooth silhouette is obvious and you can see the lack of surface imperfections up close.

3.  **Lack of Sharp, Natural Edges.** Natural terrain is formed by erosion, uplift, and fracture. This creates sharp edges, cliffs, and rocky outcroppings. The few attempts at sharp edges here still look too soft and artificial.
    *   **Where:** The edge of the dug-out hollow on the left is a uniform, soft curve. The small ledge near the blue and white pawns is a step, but its edge is perfectly straight and the corner is too neatly beveled. It looks like it was made with a "flatten" or "terrace" tool, not formed by nature.
    *   **View:** Both. The softness of the hollow's edge is clear in the high view. The artificial nature of the ledge is more apparent in the low view.

4.  **Repetitive Texture 'Stamps'.** The base grass texture is varied with patches of darker green and some small flowers. However, the pattern of these patches repeats noticeably across the map. It feels less like natural variation and more like a texture brush or procedural stamp being applied.
    *   **Where:** Look for identical clusters of darker green splotches. For example, there's a distinct three-lobed dark patch visible just to the right of the red pawn, and similar-looking patches appear elsewhere.
    *   **View:** Primarily the high view, where you can see a larger area and spot the repetition more easily.
