Yes, the land looks overtly computer-generated, smooth, and artificial. Its digital origin is immediately obvious and it does not convincingly replicate a hand-made piece of tabletop terrain.

Here are the tells, in order of how much they damage the illusion:

1.  **The Unnaturally Perfect Hill Geometry.** The single most damaging feature is the shape of the hill itself. It is a perfectly smooth, symmetrical, bell-shaped mound. Natural terrain, and even hand-sculpted wargaming terrain, is characterized by imperfections: lumps, gentler slopes, steeper cliffs, ridges, and an overall asymmetrical shape. This hill looks like it was generated by a "raise terrain" tool with a soft circular brush in a 3D editor, a purely mathematical creation.
    *   **Where:** The entire central hill.
    *   **Views:** Both. The high view reveals its perfectly round base and uniform slope. The low view shows a perfectly smooth, unbroken curve for the horizon line of the hill.

2.  **The Seamless, Uniform Transition.** The base of the hill blends into the flat ground with a perfectly consistent and gentle curve around its entire circumference. There is no variation. A real or hand-made hill would have a more complex and messy transition, with some areas being steeper, some having scree or rocks at the base, and the boundary being irregular. This perfect "falloff" screams digital generation.
    *   **Where:** The entire perimeter where the hill meets the flat ground.
    *   **Views:** Both. It's especially noticeable in the high view where the entire "footprint" of the hill is visible.

3.  **"Painted-On" Ground Textures.** The brown patches of dirt/rock are clearly flat textures applied directly to the smooth geometry. They have no depth, bumpiness, or effect on the terrain's shape. The grid lines pass over them without any deviation. They look like decals or stains rather than actual patches of different earth, which would have their own texture and subtle height differences.
    *   **Where:** The brown patches on the hill and the smaller tan patches on the surrounding plains.
    *   **Views:** Both, but it's more apparent in the low view where the lack of parallax or physical depth is easier to perceive.

4.  **The Flawlessly Deformed Grid.** While the grid is a gameplay element, the way it wraps and stretches over the hill's surface with mathematical perfection further highlights the artificiality of the underlying shape. On a physical model, a grid would be drawn or laid on, and it would likely show minor imperfections. Here, it acts like a perfect UV projection, betraying the digital mesh underneath.
    *   **Where:** The entire surface of the hill.
    *   **Views:** Both.
