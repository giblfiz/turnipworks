Yes, the land looks overwhelmingly computer-generated and artificial. The techniques used to create it have left very obvious digital artifacts.

Here are the tells, in order of how much they hurt the illusion of natural terrain:

1.  **Digital Terracing / Contour Steps:** This is the most damaging and obvious flaw. Instead of a smooth, natural slope, the hill is formed by a series of distinct, sharp-edged steps, exactly like a contour map that has been physically extruded layer by layer. Real hills are weathered and eroded into continuous curves, not stacked tiers.
    *   **Where:** This is visible across the entire surface of the hill. It is especially egregious on the main slope below the red and blue pawns in the low-angle view. You can clearly see the sharp, horizontal lines where one "layer" of the terrain ends and the next begins.
    *   **Views:** It is noticeable in the high view but is glaringly obvious and immersion-breaking in the **low view**.

2.  **Unnaturally Uniform Smoothness:** Between the contour steps mentioned above, the surface of the land is perfectly, mathematically smooth. There is no small-scale noise, no lumps, bumps, divots, or subtle undulations that characterize real ground. It looks like a piece of stretched, texture-mapped fabric.
    *   **Where:** Look at any sloped surface on the hill, particularly the large, open areas. The way the light reflects off these surfaces in the low view reveals their perfect, unblemished curvature.
    *   **Views:** **Both.** The lack of micro-detail is apparent from the high view, but the unnatural smoothness is most pronounced in the **low view**.

3.  **Repetitive Ground Texture:** The grass texture itself is tiled and repeated across the surface. While this is a common technique, the pattern is too noticeable here. There are specific dark patches and light-colored splotches that can be seen repeating across the entire map.
    *   **Where:** In the high view, look at the large flat area in the bottom left and compare it to the area in the top right. You can spot identical patterns of discoloration in the grass.
    *   **Views:** Primarily visible in the **high view** where a large area of terrain is visible at once. It's harder to spot in the low view.

4.  **Abrupt Transition from Flat to Hill:** The hill rises from the perfectly flat grid plane with no gentle, messy transition. It looks like an object that was placed *on top of* the flat ground, rather than a feature that is an integral part of it. Natural hills have a "toe" or a base that blends more gradually with the surrounding land.

    *   **Views:** **Both.**
