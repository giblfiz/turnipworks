1.  **High table-distance view:** 8/10. From a distance, it reads as a single landform due to the consistent lighting, shadow, and overall shape.
    **Low close view:** 4/10. Up close, it looks like a flat top piece placed on a set of separate, uniform slope pieces.

2.  The following seams and hard edges are visible:
    *   **Location:** The entire perimeter where the flat top surface meets the sloped sides. This is a perfectly sharp, geometric edge. **Noticeability:** Glaring, especially in the low view.
    *   **Location:** On the right side of the landform, the transition between the straight slope and the rounded corner piece. **Noticeability:** Noticeable.
    *   **Location:** On the left side, where the slope curves inward. The change in the slope's angle suggests a seam between two different pieces. **Noticeability:** Subtle.

3.  The grass colours and textures do not fully match.
    *   **Hue:** The green hue is consistent between the slopes and the flat ground.
    *   **Brightness:** The flat top of the raised land is noticeably brighter and more saturated than the surrounding flat ground, which it should not be. The slopes are appropriately darker due to shadow.
    *   **Texture Scale:** The grass/dirt texture on the slopes appears stretched and lower in resolution compared to the texture on the flat ground.

4.  The shape reads as geometric and man-made, like a stylized plateau or earthwork, not a natural hill. The single biggest thing that breaks the illusion is the unnaturally sharp, hard edge between the perfectly flat top and the uniformly angled slopes. Natural terrain has a softer, rounded transition at the shoulder of a hill or edge of a plateau.

5.  *   **Blue pawn:** Sits convincingly. Its base is perfectly flat on the top surface.
    *   **Yellow pawn:** Sits convincingly. Its base is also perfectly flat on the top surface.
    *   **White figure:** Does not sit convincingly. It is on the slope, and its flat base is visibly cutting into the hillside on its upper edge.

6.  **At the high table-distance view only:** Yes, a player would accept this as "a hill / plateau" because it clearly communicates the game mechanic of an elevated area with distinct boundaries.

VERDICT: mostly continuous with visible seams.
