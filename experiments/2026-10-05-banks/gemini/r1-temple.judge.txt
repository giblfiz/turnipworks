**1. Landform Continuity Score**
*   **High table-distance view:** 4/10. The overall shape is coherent, but the straight edges, gridded texture, and visible lines between sections make it look like a constructed model rather than a natural landform.
*   **Low close view:** 1/10. From this angle, it clearly reads as separate, blocky objects with flat vertical sides placed next to each other. The modular construction is completely exposed.

**2. Seams, Gaps, and Hard Edges**
*   **Between all tiered sections:** There are glaring, hard 90-degree edges and flat vertical faces where each tier of the hill is stacked. These read as cut faces, not natural slopes.
*   **Around the stairs:** There are noticeable gaps on either side of both sets of stairs, showing they are separate objects inserted into the terrain blocks.
*   **Between corner and straight sections:** On the lowest tier at the front right, a subtle vertical seam is visible where a straight wall piece meets a corner piece. A similar noticeable seam exists on the middle tier where the hedge-topped bank meets the main hill mass.
*   **Across the top plateau:** In the high view, a subtle horizontal seam is visible across the front of the highest level, just below the temple structure, indicating the front face is a separate piece from the top surface.

**3. Grass Colour and Texture Match**
The hue of the green grass is consistent across the flat ground and the top surfaces of the raised land. However, the brightness and texture do not match. The top surfaces of the hill appear slightly darker than the surrounding ground. Most significantly, the texture on the vertical "cut faces" of the hill sections is stretched, smeared, and low-resolution, making it obvious that a flat ground texture has been improperly applied to a vertical surface.

**4. Believability of Shape**
The shape reads as geometric and man-made, like a ziggurat or terraced earthworks, not a natural hill.
The single biggest thing that breaks the illusion is the presence of perfectly flat, vertical faces on the sides of each terrain piece. Natural terrain erodes into slopes; these look like they were cut with a saw.

**5. Pawn Placement on Raised Land**
*   **Yellow pawn:** Sits convincingly on the flat top surface.
*   **Blue pawn:** Sits unconvincingly. It is on a slope, causing its base to clip into the hill on the uphill side and float slightly above it on the downhill side.
*   **Red pawn:** Sits unconvincingly. Like the blue pawn, its base is not flush with the slight slope, cutting into the ground.
*   **White figure:** Sits convincingly on a relatively flat part of the middle tier.

**6. Player Acceptance (High View)**
Mostly. A player would accept it as a "hill" for gameplay purposes because its tiered levels clearly communicate height and changes in elevation at a glance.

VERDICT: separate objects.
