# Turnip Works: product directions (the parking lot)

This mirrors the wiki node `<private wiki>`. It is where "it should also…" ideas go, so they're captured without leaking into the tools. The Spawn Tuner is good enough that Harry keeps wanting to turn it into the product. He reads that as a good sign and a wrong impulse (2026-10-01). Ideas land here with a date and their source, and get promoted into SPEC.md only when the product build actually starts. The repo mirror is `docs/PRODUCT_DIRECTIONS.md` in ``.

**Harry's list, 2026-10-01** (after a chat about features for the final product):

1. **A GET-only LLM API for GMs.** A GM partners with *any* chat LLM, down to the weakest free ChatGPT window, with no MCP and no tool-calling: the LLM follows a pasted rules card and fetches plain URLs. Reads return compact readable text or JSON (the map, a room, what's at a coordinate). Writes are also GETs (add, move, describe, roll) carrying a per-table capability token, with a server-side undo log and rate limits. This fits the architecture: the AI writes recipes, fixed solvers do geometry. **Built** (2026-10-02 onward, docs/NETCODE.md "LLM participants"); brought up to date 2026-10-20 with a short card, help pages and live trials on a small model (experiments/2026-10-20-llm-api/). **Harry's clarification:** the worry is *tool access*, not model intelligence. Lots of people only use chat-window LLMs; the Law Library hit the same wall. The deeper principle is loose coupling: don't learn the bitter lesson again by building anything tightly coupled to today's models or protocols. Plain URLs that any model can fetch will outlive any specific integration.
2. **The prop assembler.** A user-facing sub-window for building small custom combinations of props (a dressed table, a loot pile, an altar arrangement). It's the user face of our render sets (`catalog/sets.md`), and can bake down to one mesh for draw calls.
3. **Keeping players busy between turns:**
   - **Environmental toys:** fog, lighting and water that players can play in and with.
   - **Free object movement for players,** with the GM keeping good undo and a record of every move. The Spawn Tuner's move/turn recording (2026-10-01) is a prototype of the mechanism only. **Harry: the GM UX is brutal.** A frazzled GM won't read a log; undo and awareness have to be *visual* (to be designed when we get there).
   - **A "home castle":** a persistent player-owned space they build on freely between sessions.
   - **Dice-stacking games.**
   - **Prop-assembler access** for players.

**Flavors** (Harry, 2026-10-01): the default is D&D fantasy (player expectation over history). A strict historic flavor, Wild West or sci-fi are possible but far off, roughly equally so. This is why room rules target roles rather than objects: a flavor = asset pack + rule overrides on one engine.

**Captured from the tuner as product candidates (Fauna):** the role/zone/feng-shui layout engine (`tools/spawn-tuner/ROOMS.md`); Harry's spawn-in/out presets (harry-v2); per-object notes and moves as a GM ↔ AI feedback channel.


**Sharing builds: export, import, a community library** (Harry, 2026-10-15: "stupid obvious but we absolutely need… I can build a really great wizard's tower, and other people can use it"). On the roadmap, not started. What exists: Settings ▸ Campaign admin exports a JSON of every area the seat can see and imports them as new areas (docs/UI.md "Campaign admin"); players may ask the GM to import/export. Fauna's sketch of the rest, in three steps:
1. **Share a selection, not just a campaign.** Export/import at the grain people brag about: an area, a building, a room, a prop arrangement (the prop assembler's output). The file is recipes + library references, not meshes, so it is small; a version field, and a stand-in for any piece the importer's library lacks. GM-only parts (hidden rooms, monsters, traps, GM notes) travel by default, since the recipient is a GM, behind a "strip secrets" box; never seats, chat, explored fog or player data. Plus "copy share link": an unlisted hosted copy anyone can import from. This step needs no accounts or moderation, and it is also the alpha's starter kit (a demo tower or dungeon is just a shared build).
2. **The library site.** Browse by the 3/4 picture the game already renders, tags (room type, faction, size, storeys), favourites and ratings, and "Use in my campaign". Posting needs a light identity (a handle tied to the GM's seat; no full accounts yet), a licence choice per upload (suggested CC-BY), and a report button.
3. **User uploads** (their own meshes, images) inside shared builds. This is where safe harbour (docs/IMPORTS.md section 7) stops being theoretical, because a public workshop is exactly where every known takedown landed. Before step 2 is public: a registered DMCA designated agent, a takedown address, a repeat-infringer policy, and an "I have the right to share this" line in the terms; Fauna runs takedowns. Recipes built only from TW's own library carry no third-party risk.

**Real dice** (Harry, 2026-10-15): "We don't have 'dice', we have 'roll'." Physical 3D dice that tumble onto the table for everyone, the "ohhh" moment: "such a dumb feature and people love it so much." Shape to work out: the relay decides the result (as rolls do now) and the animation lands on it; painted house-style dice; who sees a hidden roll's dice. Atlas VTT (Obsidian) shipped the same as a popular add-on. **Built 2026-10-19** (docs/STATUS.md "Real 3D dice"): rounded painted dice on the table, the Spawn Tuner's Dice panel. Still open: hidden (GM) and whisper rolls (chat has no hidden roll yet), sound.

**Music and ambience** (Harry, 2026-10-15): he can hand over background music. The engine has no audio yet (Settings ▸ Me stores volumes that do nothing). Likely shape: a GM playlist that plays for every seat, per-area ambience, each person's own volume.
