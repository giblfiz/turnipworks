Decisions worth keeping. **Buildings:** "realistic vs gamist" is three independent dials per type — circulation (tree ↔ loops), what decides adjacency (function ↔ pacing), envelope (packed ↔ carved) — served by two seeded solvers; the stair core is placed first, one or two per building, aligned through storeys; storey count is a per-type default changed by contextual buttons. Explicit dungeon pacing is not modelled yet (question open with Harry). **Flavors:** symbol-themes, not named gods, filling a fixed slot set (altar centrepiece, idol, banner, wall motif, floor sigil, censer) so a recipe swaps family only; Harry: a handful of assets per flavor, mostly 2D banners and floor tiles, one or two centrepieces. List: sun, moon, nature, sword & shield; spiders, dragon (plain, not five-headed), devils, skulls & skeletons, tentacles; plus Fauna's additions Harry liked: a respectable death flavor, scales-and-coin, forge-and-hammer, sea/storm. **Types:** Harry asked for every remaining fantasy type; wave one is wizard's tower, cult sanctum (devils, spiders), temple (sun, moon), dungeon, market & village; then farmstead, smithy, mine, lair; then ruins (possibly a variant of every type rather than a type — unsettled), camp, grove.

**Big maps ("mostly nothing"), agreed in conversation, not yet built:** the goal is that users can build large maps freely and most of it costs nearly nothing, rather than guiding them to small maps. The earlier sleeping-rooms mechanism only sleeps rooms far from every view, so a zoomed-out view of a town is fully live (Harry: it was "very expensive", not "mostly nothing"). Design: every building/room has a LID and three states per viewer — closed (shell only: walls + roof, shared/instanced; no interior props, lights or fog; lit windows are a painted glow), warm (interior on the GPU, not drawn), open (drawn). A few open, a handful warm, the rest closed, whatever the map size; the least recently used warm interior drops to closed (Harry's "discardable" idea); a cold building opens under cover of the spawn animation and is warmed ahead as the camera or party approaches. Open/closed is PER VIEWER (Harry: "we don't all look at the same thing"). Dungeons get lids too (Harry: "who says this needs to be the case? What if we just make dungeon roofs?"): a rock cap per room; and, accepted by Harry ("I'm game for the 'floor plan on the cap'"), the floor plan is drawn on the cap so a zoomed-out dungeon reads as a map. Three player states follow: never discovered = plain rock; seen before, not there now = the drawn plan on the cap; where your pawn is = open. Also agreed today: selection glow is cut; water passes run only when real bodies of water are in view; fog and fire stay on toggles; lighting gets separate switches (flame lights, their shadows, key-light shadow, contact shadows) so their visual weight can be judged; Shift = snap to steps on every ring handle (free by default); hand-painted floor tiles survive a whole-floor drop; edge-pan also while dragging rectangles.

Still to talk out: textures and compression, roofs-on below the cut, not drawing below the current level.

## 2026-09-30 — noncommercial worker dependencies retired
